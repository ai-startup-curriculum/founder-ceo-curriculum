# Exercise 01 — Customer Discovery Sprint

**Module:** 001 Customer Discovery · **Stage:** IDEA→PRE-SEED · **Time:** ~1
week (≈6–8 focused hours) · **Prereq exercises:** ideally
[exercise-03 segment narrowing](./exercise-03-segment-narrowing-drill.md)
and [exercise-02 interview guide authoring](./exercise-02-mom-test-interview-guide-authoring.md)
first — this sprint puts them into contact with real (or realistically
simulated) people.

## Problem statement

You have a candidate problem hypothesis and no evidence yet — only your
belief that the problem is real, urgent, and paid for. This exercise makes
you find out, cheaply, in the shape of a one-week sprint that produces two
founder artifacts and one decision.

By the end of the week you'll have run 15–20 interviews (or, on the
simulated path, at least 5 realistic role-played ones) inside a single
narrow segment, synthesised the results into a memo a skeptic could read,
and committed to a written **go / pivot / kill** call with the
disconfirming evidence that would flip it.

## Deliverables (two founder artifacts + one decision)

1. **A discovery plan** — a one-sentence segment definition, a recruit list
   of 15–20 targets with channels, and an interview guide of 5–8 open,
   past-tense, non-leading questions that pass the Mom Test rules (see
   [chapter 02](../02-mom-test-interviews.md)). The plan is the artifact
   you would defensibly hand to a co-founder before the interviews begin.
2. **An insight memo** — a synthesis across your interviews, structured
   per [chapter 05](../05-insight-memo-synthesis.md): header, counted
   patterns (with signal audits from
   [chapter 03](../03-signals-a-problem-is-real.md)), surprises and
   disconfirming evidence, decision, what would change it, method note.
   Roughly one to four pages.
3. **A written go / pivot / kill call**, in one sentence at the end of the
   memo, with the disconfirming evidence named per
   [chapter 06](../06-go-pivot-kill.md).

A worked reference for the deliverables lives in
[`exemplars/mod-001-customer-discovery/`](../../../exemplars/mod-001-customer-discovery/).
Compare method and rigor, not wording — your segment, questions, and
conclusion will differ.

## Scenario

Pick one:

- **Your real startup** (recommended if you're an operating founder): take
  your single riskiest **problem hypothesis** and run this on it this
  week. If you don't have one written down as *who / when / outcome /
  signals* (chapter 01), write it before you start.
- **Simulated:** you believe *"first-time technical founders in the six
  months after incorporation experience simultaneous, unordered exposure
  to fundraising, corporate law, equity, and GTM decisions, and lack a
  stage-appropriate map of what to learn when."* Test the **problem**
  (overwhelm / lack of a map), not the solution (a curriculum). Where the
  real path calls for interviews, the simulated path runs at least 5
  realistic role-played interviews and notes where the sprint would
  expand.

## Requirements

The sprint has to satisfy each of the following, checkably, by the end of
the week:

- **Segment is one recognisable sentence.** Not "founders." Not "SMBs."
  A filter you could apply to LinkedIn and to a warm intro request and
  get a manageable, coherent set of names back. See
  [chapter 04](../04-sizing-and-segmenting.md).
- **Interview guide is Mom-Test clean.** Every question is open, past-tense,
  and non-leading, and would still make sense if the buyer had never
  heard of you. Include at least one *"walk me through the last time…"*
  and one *"what have you already tried or paid for?"*
- **Recruit list has 15–20 named targets** (or, for simulated, at least
  5 realistic role-play personas mapped to real archetypes you could
  eventually reach). Note the channel per target.
- **Interviews are captured per the template below**, immediately after
  each call, in the buyer's own words where possible. Segment-fit is
  recorded as a field per interview.
- **Synthesis is counted, not curated.** Patterns are named with
  unprompted-interview counts (e.g. *12/16*), then signal-audited against
  the four signals from chapter 03.
- **Surprises are named as a section.** Off-segment findings,
  pattern-breakers, and unrelated pain the buyers surfaced spontaneously.
- **Decision is one sentence.** Go / pivot / kill, followed by 2–4
  bulleted observations that would change the call.

## Starter guidance

**Day 1 — plan.** Write the problem hypothesis in the *who / when /
outcome / signals* shape. Narrow the segment to a one-sentence, recognisable
filter (do exercise 03 first if you haven't). Draft the interview guide (5–8
questions, do exercise 02 first if you haven't). Build the recruit list —
plan a ~7–10× funnel from ask to usable interview.

**Days 2–5 — run.** Fire warm-intro asks first, then community posts, then
cold outreach against public signals. Schedule 20–30 minute calls. Aim to
run 3–5 per day; more than that and the notes suffer. Capture per the
template inside the same hour as the call — memory decays quickly.

**Day 6 — synthesise.** Sort notes by segment fit, tag each with 1–3
candidate pattern labels, merge and count, signal-audit the top patterns
(see chapter 05's one-hour drill).

**Day 7 — write and decide.** Write the memo. End with the go / pivot /
kill sentence and the disconfirming-evidence bullets. Share with a
co-founder or peer and let them poke holes; the exercise isn't done until
the memo survives a skeptical read.

## Per-interview capture template

```
Interview date:
Person (masked ID) / role / company shape:
Segment fit (does the person match the segment definition? yes/no/adjacent):
Specific recent story (with detail, dates, quantities):
What they've already tried / spent (time or money, named vendors if any):
Quantified pain (money, time, headcount, opportunity, if any):
Existing workarounds (spreadsheets, hacks, misused tools, manual work):
Surprises / disconfirming evidence:
Direct quotes worth keeping (short, in their words):
Follow-ups they'd take (intro to X, willing to try prototype, etc.):
```

## Acceptance criteria

You are done — and this sprint has met the module's standard — when *all*
of the following are true:

- [ ] The **segment** is one sentence, recognisable, and you can name three
      real people who fit it.
- [ ] The **interview guide** contains 5–8 questions, every one of which
      passes a Mom Test self-check (past-tense, behavioral, non-leading,
      not a pitch).
- [ ] You ran (or realistically role-played) **at least the minimum
      interview count** — 15 for the real path, 5 for the simulated
      path — and captured each per the template.
- [ ] The **insight memo** has all six sections: header, counted patterns,
      surprises, decision, what would change it, method note.
- [ ] Each **pattern is stated with a count** of the interviews (unprompted)
      that support it and a signal audit against the four chapter-03
      signals.
- [ ] The memo ends in **one sentence naming the go / pivot / kill call**
      and lists **2–4 pieces of disconfirming evidence** that would flip it.
- [ ] A skeptical co-founder or peer reading the memo would either agree
      the call is supported, or point to exactly which evidence is missing —
      not shrug.

## Self-assessment rubric

| Dimension | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Segment | "Founders" | Narrow, recognisable, one sentence; three real names on hand |
| Questions | Leading / hypothetical ("Would you use…?") | Past-tense, behavioral, Mom-Test clean; make sense with no product context |
| Recruit list | "My network" as one line | 15–20 named targets, per-target channel, planned to ~7–10× funnel |
| Capture | Paraphrased summaries, missing fields | Structured per template, in buyer's words, filled same-day |
| Evidence | Opinions & compliments | Stories + prior spend + workarounds + quantified cost, unprompted |
| Synthesis | Cherry-picked favourite quote | Counted patterns across the set, signal-audited |
| Surprises | Absent | Own section, pattern-breakers + unrelated pain named |
| Decision | "Everyone loved it, go!" or unwritten | One-sentence call, 2–4 pieces of named disconfirming evidence |

## Definition of done

A skeptical co-founder reading your insight memo would agree that your go
/ pivot / kill call is supported by the evidence you gathered — or could
point to *exactly* which evidence is missing. In either case the sprint
has done its job: it has turned a private belief into a public artifact
you can be held to.
