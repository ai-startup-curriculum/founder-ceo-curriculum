# Exercise 02 — Authoring a Mom-Test-Clean Interview Guide

**Module:** 001 Customer Discovery · **Stage:** IDEA→PRE-SEED · **Time:**
~2 focused hours · **Fits before:** [exercise-01 discovery sprint](./exercise-01-discovery-sprint.md).

## Problem statement

An interview is only as honest as the questions in it. Nearly every
first-draft interview guide a founder writes is full of hypothetical,
leading, or solution-oriented questions — and any interview that runs on
those questions returns compliments rather than evidence. This exercise
makes you author a 5–8 question guide, run it through the Mom Test, and
rewrite until every question passes.

The deliverable is small (one page). The value is that when you sit down
to interview real people in [exercise-01](./exercise-01-discovery-sprint.md),
you are asking questions whose answers you can trust.

## Deliverable (one artifact)

A one-page **interview guide** with:

- A one-sentence **framing intro** you'd read at the start of the call
  ("I'm not selling anything…").
- **5–8 open, past-tense, non-leading questions** in the order you'd ask
  them, each annotated with the Mom Test rule(s) it satisfies (see
  [chapter 02](../02-mom-test-interviews.md)).
- A short **self-check table** applying the four Mom Test criteria to
  every question (see the rubric below).
- A pair of **before/after rewrites** — three of your own first-draft
  hypothetical questions and the past-tense rewrite of each — attached at
  the bottom of the guide. This is the audit trail that shows you did the
  work, not just wrote a clean final draft.

## Scenario

Pick one:

- **Your real startup**: use the problem hypothesis you'd take into
  [exercise-01](./exercise-01-discovery-sprint.md). The guide you produce
  should be the guide you'd actually run this week.
- **Simulated:** use the module's exemplar problem hypothesis —
  *"first-time technical founders in the six months after incorporation
  experience simultaneous, unordered exposure to fundraising, corporate
  law, equity, and GTM decisions."* The segment is "first-time technical
  founders who incorporated and began raising a pre-seed in the last
  twelve months."

Either way, the guide's questions must be about **the buyer's life and
past behavior** — not about the product you might eventually sell them.

## Requirements

The guide has to satisfy each of the following:

- **5–8 questions.** Fewer and you won't fill 20–30 minutes; more and the
  interview becomes a checklist rather than a conversation.
- **Every question is open-ended.** No yes/no. No two-option choices. A
  question that can be answered "yes" was a bad question.
- **Every question is past-tense or about the present.** No hypotheticals
  ("would you…?", "if we built…?", "how likely are you to…?"). The one
  exception is the closing hand-off ("what haven't I asked that I
  should?") which is a meta-question about the interview itself, not
  about the product.
- **At least one question is a "walk me through the last time…"** — the
  behavioral anchor question that produces the specific recent story
  from [chapter 03](../03-signals-a-problem-is-real.md).
- **At least one question elicits prior spend or a workaround** —
  "what have you already tried, bought, or built to deal with this?"
- **No question mentions your product, category, or hypothesised
  solution.** The buyer should be able to answer every question without
  ever knowing you were considering building anything.
- **The guide would still make sense** if the buyer had never heard of
  you. This is the Mom Test's "your mom could answer this without
  flattering you" property, made operational.

## Starter guidance

**Step 1 — Read chapter 02.** Especially the "behavioral rewrite" table
and the failure modes. It's five minutes and it will pay for itself.

**Step 2 — Draft ugly.** Write 8–12 first-draft questions without
editing. Include the hypothetical ones you *know* are bad; the rewrite
in step 4 is where the value comes from.

**Step 3 — Sort and cut.** Group the drafts by what they're trying to
learn (context / trigger / current process / cost / prior spend /
close). Keep the strongest one or two per group; cut the rest.

**Step 4 — Rewrite hypothetical to behavioral.** For every remaining
question, apply the table in [chapter 02](../02-mom-test-interviews.md).
"Would you use…?" becomes "walk me through the last time…." "Do you
think X is important?" becomes "tell me about the last time X came
up." Preserve at least three before/after pairs — those are part of the
deliverable.

**Step 5 — Run the self-check.** For each surviving question, fill in
the four Mom Test criteria in the table below. Any question failing one
gets rewritten or dropped.

**Step 6 — Order the questions.** Broad context first, specific stories
next, prior spend and cost in the middle, closing hand-off last. Do not
lead with "what have you paid for?" — the buyer needs the context
questions to warm up first.

**Step 7 — Dry-run with one person.** Read the guide aloud to a
co-founder, a friend, or into a voice memo. Any question that feels
awkward to say out loud will feel worse to answer. Rewrite the awkward
ones.

## Self-check table template (fill in per question)

```
| # | Question                       | About their life? | Past-tense? | Open? | Would make sense with no product context? |
|---|--------------------------------|-------------------|-------------|-------|--------------------------------------------|
| 1 | Walk me through the last time…  | Yes              | Yes         | Yes   | Yes                                        |
| 2 | ...                            | ...              | ...         | ...   | ...                                        |
```

Any "No" in any column is a rewrite trigger.

## Before/after rewrite template (attach 3 pairs at the bottom of the guide)

```
Before (draft): "Would you use a tool that helps you [X]?"
After (rewrite): "Walk me through the last time you tried to [X].
                   What did you actually do?"
Why the rewrite passes: past-tense, about their life, would still make
                        sense if they'd never heard of me.
```

## Acceptance criteria

You are done when *all* of the following are true:

- [ ] The guide contains **5–8 questions**, plus a framing intro and a
      closing hand-off.
- [ ] Every question passes the **four-column Mom Test self-check**.
- [ ] At least one question is **"walk me through the last time…"**.
- [ ] At least one question elicits **prior spend or a workaround** in
      past-tense form.
- [ ] **No question mentions** your product, category, or hypothesised
      solution.
- [ ] The guide includes **at least three before/after rewrites** of your
      own first-draft hypothetical questions.
- [ ] Reading the guide aloud to a co-founder or peer produces
      "yes, I'd answer these honestly" — not "wait, are you selling me
      something?"

## Self-assessment rubric

| Dimension | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Question count | 3 or 12+ | 5–8, each earning its slot |
| Question shape | Hypothetical / leading / yes-no | Past-tense, behavioral, open, non-leading |
| Product neutrality | Product / category mentioned | Guide reads as pure life-and-past interview |
| Signal coverage | Only context questions | Includes story, prior spend, cost, workaround prompts |
| Ordering | Random / prior spend first | Context → story → prior spend / cost → hand-off |
| Rewrite trail | None (only final draft shown) | 3+ before/after pairs demonstrating the work |
| Dry-run | Never read aloud | Read aloud and awkward wording fixed |

## Definition of done

A co-founder or peer could take your guide, walk into a 30-minute
interview with a segment-fit buyer, and come back with notes containing
the four chapter-03 signals — because the questions surfaced them, not
because the co-founder is a talented interviewer. If the guide only works
in your hands, it's not done yet.
