# Chapter 07 — Boundary: This Module Owns the First Sprint; the CPO Track Owns the Discipline

> **Reads with:** module objective 7 — *cite the boundary to `cpo-curriculum`
> for deep ongoing discovery / continuous-discovery-habits craft — this
> module owns the first discovery sprint, the CPO track owns the discipline
> as a permanent function.*

## Why the boundary exists

Founder/CEO curriculum modules are role pathways over shared functional
curricula, not a separate body of knowledge (see `README.md` and
`PREREQUISITES.md`). The founder learns *enough of every function* to run
the company; the deeper craft of each function is owned by the pathway for
the role that will eventually do it full-time. Customer discovery follows
the same rule.

- **This module (`founder-ceo/mod-001-customer-discovery`)** teaches you to
  run the *first* discovery sprint — the one that decides whether an idea
  survives contact with a real segment before you commit the next block of
  capital and calendar to it. You leave this module able to plan, run,
  synthesise, and defend one sprint end to end.
- **The CPO track (`cpo-curriculum`)** teaches customer discovery as a
  **permanent function** of the product organisation: continuous discovery
  habits, opportunity solution trees, story mapping, assumption testing at
  scale, discovery / delivery integration, and the operational patterns
  that keep discovery load-balanced with shipping.

If you are the founder-CEO of a pre-product-hire company, both are your
job today, and you should read both. Once a product hire is in seat, the
sustained-discovery discipline transfers to them, and this module's
material becomes what you use to *supervise*, not to *practice*.

## What "the first sprint" covers, and what it deliberately doesn't

This module is scoped to a one-week (~6–8 focused-hour) sprint that
produces two artifacts — a discovery plan and an insight memo — and one
decision (go / pivot / kill). That scope is deliberate. It matches the
moment in a founder's life when they need to know, cheaply and quickly,
whether the problem they are about to build against is real.

Explicitly **in** scope for this module:

- Framing a problem hypothesis as distinct from a solution hypothesis
  (chapter 01).
- The Mom Test interview discipline for one sprint (chapter 02).
- Recognising the four signals of a real problem (chapter 03).
- Narrowing a segment and sizing a sprint (chapter 04).
- Synthesising a set of interviews into an insight memo (chapter 05).
- Making a defensible go / pivot / kill call (chapter 06).

Explicitly **out** of scope for this module — deferred to the CPO track:

- **Continuous discovery habits.** The weekly cadence of touchpoints with
  customers that a shipping product team runs indefinitely. Teresa
  Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits* (2021), is the reference.
- **Opportunity solution trees.** The structured artifact for mapping
  outcomes to opportunities to solutions to experiments, and using it as
  a live planning tool over time.
- **Assumption mapping and testing at scale.** Systematically breaking a
  bet into its underlying assumptions and running the cheapest experiment
  per assumption; runs in parallel with delivery.
- **Discovery / delivery integration.** How discovery work sits inside a
  product team's operating cadence, how discovery insight becomes
  roadmap, how the discovery function is staffed and reviewed.
- **Research operations (ResearchOps).** Recruit panels, incentive
  management, insight repositories, and the tooling that supports
  research as a permanent function.
- **Deeper research methods.** Diary studies, ethnographic observation,
  survey design with statistical rigor, quantitative behavioral analysis
  layered onto qualitative research. Discovery beyond interviews.

The distinction isn't sharp on any single question — a founder running the
first sprint touches, in miniature, most of what a CPO does at scale — but
it is sharp enough at the *cadence and infrastructure* level to justify a
handoff. One sprint is the founder's job; the sprint becoming a
never-ending function is the CPO's.

## When to graduate from this module to the CPO track

Three signals it's time to move discovery work from "founder does it
between everything else" to "product function owns it":

- **Product hire in seat.** As soon as you have a first product manager
  or head of product, continuous discovery is theirs to build. Hand off
  the artifacts (this module's memo and its per-interview notes) as the
  starting corpus.
- **The company has shipped.** Once there is a product in the hands of
  users, the shape of discovery changes — you're now testing changes to
  a live experience, not the existence of the pain. The CPO track's
  methods (opportunity solution trees, assumption testing) start to earn
  their keep.
- **The pattern of "one big sprint every six months" is failing.** If
  discovery has become episodic and the memos are aging faster than the
  market is telling you they should be, you need continuous discovery,
  not a bigger sprint.

Before those signals, this module's material is sufficient. After them,
this module's material remains true — the Mom Test never stops being
right — but the operating pattern it lives inside is different, and that
is what the CPO track teaches.

## What this module's founder still owes discovery, always

The boundary transfers the *craft* to the CPO track. It does not transfer
the *judgment* — that is permanently CEO work. The founder-CEO's ongoing
discovery obligation, at every stage of the company, is:

- **Talk to customers directly, at a floor cadence.** Even after the
  product function is staffed, the CEO stays in real customer
  conversations frequently enough to notice when the memo starts to lie.
  A useful floor is "at least one real customer conversation per week
  the CEO isn't selling in." The specific number matters less than the
  refusal to lose the muscle.
- **Own the segment framing.** The CEO decides who the company is for.
  That decision is fed by discovery but not delegated to it.
- **Read the discovery output as a first-class strategic input.** The
  CPO's continuous discovery output feeds strategy and capital allocation
  — CEO-only decisions. A CEO who has stopped reading discovery has
  outsourced the shape of the company.

The material in this module is the founder-CEO's baseline discovery
literacy. It is designed to be re-read whenever a fresh problem
hypothesis appears — a new product line, a new segment, an acquired
company — because every new hypothesis deserves the same first-sprint
discipline the original one got.

## Summary

- This module ends at the **first sprint**. You leave it able to plan,
  run, and decide on one discovery cycle end to end.
- The **continuous discovery discipline** — cadence, opportunity solution
  trees, assumption testing, research operations, discovery/delivery
  integration — is owned by the CPO track. Defer to it once a product
  function exists.
- Transfer *craft* to the CPO track; keep *judgment* (segment, strategy,
  personal customer contact) permanently with the CEO.
- Re-run this module's method on every new problem hypothesis the
  company acquires. The Mom Test doesn't get stale; your certainty about
  a new market does.
