# Resources — Module 001 Customer Discovery

Real, citable references for the material in this module. Primary sources
are listed first per topic; where secondary sources are useful for
narrower application (e.g., worked examples, community write-ups), they
follow.

## Books (primary references)

- **Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test: How to talk to customers & learn if
  your business is a good idea when everyone is lying to you* (2013).**
  The canonical short book on discovery interviews and the source of
  the three rules used in [chapter 02](./02-mom-test-interviews.md).
  Author's site: [momtestbook.com](https://momtestbook.com/).
- **Steve Blank, *The Four Steps to the Epiphany: Successful Strategies
  for Products that Win* (2005; revised editions since).** The origin
  of the customer-development model and the customer-discovery /
  customer-validation distinction that underpins
  [chapter 01](./01-problem-vs-solution-hypothesis.md). Blank's
  ongoing writing at [steveblank.com](https://steveblank.com/) tracks
  updates to the model.
- **Steve Blank & Bob Dorf, *The Startup Owner's Manual: The Step-by-
  Step Guide for Building a Great Company* (2012).** The operationalised
  manual for customer development, including step-by-step discovery
  and validation checklists.
- **Eric Ries, *The Lean Startup: How Today's Entrepreneurs Use
  Continuous Innovation to Create Radically Successful Businesses*
  (2011).** The value-hypothesis / growth-hypothesis framing referenced
  in [chapter 01](./01-problem-vs-solution-hypothesis.md), plus the
  build-measure-learn loop that customer discovery instantiates.
- **Ash Maurya, *Running Lean: Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works*
  (multiple editions; latest 3rd edition, 2022).** Practical application
  of Blank + Ries to a real founder's schedule, including problem and
  solution interview templates.
- **Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits: Discover Products That
  Create Customer Value and Business Value* (2021).** The reference for
  the discipline this module hands off to the CPO track in
  [chapter 07](./07-boundary-cpo-continuous-discovery.md). Ongoing
  writing at [producttalk.org](https://www.producttalk.org/).
- **Alberto Savoia, *The Right It: Why So Many Ideas Fail and How to
  Make Sure Yours Succeed* (2019).** Pretotyping and market-engagement
  hypotheses — useful complement to interview-only discovery for
  founders willing to run cheap live experiments.

## Job-to-be-Done reading (segmentation on the axis that matters)

- **Clayton Christensen, Karen Dillon, Taddy Hall & David Duncan,
  *Competing Against Luck: The Story of Innovation and Customer Choice*
  (2016).** The book-length treatment of Jobs-to-be-Done and the
  reframing of segmentation from demographic to
  situation/outcome-based.
- **Tony Ulwick, *Jobs to be Done: Theory to Practice* (2016).** The
  Outcome-Driven Innovation formulation of JTBD, including "job
  statements" and desired-outcome statements as an alternative to the
  interview-only approach.
- **Clayton Christensen, "Know Your Customers' 'Jobs to Be Done'"
  (Harvard Business Review, September 2016).** A short introduction to
  the core JTBD reframing; useful as a first read.

## Qualitative research methods (sample size and saturation)

- **Greg Guest, Arwen Bunce & Laura Johnson, "How Many Interviews Are
  Enough? An Experiment with Data Saturation and Variability," *Field
  Methods* 18(1): 59–82 (2006).** The most-cited empirical study on
  qualitative-interview saturation and the source of the ~12-interview
  heuristic used in [chapter 04](./04-sizing-and-segmenting.md).
  <!-- needs-research: DOI / stable URL — commonly cited as doi:10.1177/1525822X05279903. -->
- **Jakob Nielsen, "Why You Only Need to Test with 5 Users" (Nielsen
  Norman Group, 2000).** The complementary usability-research finding
  that small samples surface most patterns when the population is
  homogeneous — useful counterpoint on why segment narrowing matters
  more than sample size.
  <!-- needs-research: primary URL — nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/. -->

## Founder-facing writing on discovery

- **Y Combinator, *Startup School* library
  ([startupschool.org](https://www.startupschool.org/)).** Free
  lectures on customer discovery from repeated YC batches; especially
  Michael Seibel and Kevin Hale on "how to talk to users."
- **Y Combinator blog, "How to Talk to Users" (Eric Migicovsky
  lecture).** Applied Mom Test rules for early-stage YC startups.
  <!-- needs-research: primary URL — commonly hosted at ycombinator.com or youtube.com/@ycombinator. -->
- **First Round Review** ([firstround.com/review](https://firstround.com/review/)).
  Long-form operator interviews; several essays on customer discovery
  practice from founders who have run it end-to-end.
- **Andreessen Horowitz Growth & Product content**
  ([a16z.com](https://a16z.com/)). Occasional deep essays on customer
  research and product-market fit measurement; look for pieces by
  Sarah Tavel and Andrew Chen on demand signals.
- **Reforge blog** ([reforge.com/blog](https://www.reforge.com/blog)).
  Continuous-discovery, activation, and retention writing that
  connects the module material to the practice of a growing product
  team.

## Notes on citation hygiene

Where a specific claim in this module cites a fact that would benefit
from a primary source and I could not verify one at authoring time, the
chapter has an inline `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` comment marking
the gap. These do not block reading; they mark items for later
verification and block the automated merge pipeline until resolved. If
you spot a citation that should be present or a source that has been
superseded, opening an issue against this repository is the fastest way
to fix it.
