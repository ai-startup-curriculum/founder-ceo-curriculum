## Books (primary references)

- **Brad Feld & Jason Mendelson, *Venture Deals: Be Smarter Than Your
  Lawyer and Venture Capitalist* (Wiley, multiple editions; 4th ed.
  2019).** The reference on the mechanics of a round — term-sheet
  economics, option-pool math, control provisions — and what
  different runway targets imply for dilution and control. Directly
  informs the "what a raise buys" discussion referenced from
  [chapter 06](./06-decision-months-on-the-runway-curve.md).
  <!-- needs-research: ISBN and stable URL for Venture Deals, current edition. -->
- **Bill Janeway, *Doing Capitalism in the Innovation Economy:
  Markets, Speculation and the State* (Cambridge University Press,
  2nd ed. 2018).** The longer argument for why cash-cycle discipline
  is what separates surviving startups from clever ones. Historical
  context for the raise-by / cut-by / kill-by discipline in
  [chapter 06](./06-decision-months-on-the-runway-curve.md).
- **Eric Ries, *The Lean Startup: How Today's Entrepreneurs Use
  Continuous Innovation to Create Radically Successful Businesses*
  (Crown Business, 2011).** The validated-learning framing that
  underpins why the operating model has to be a *live* instrument
  ([chapter 04](./04-eighteen-month-operating-model.md)) rather than
  a static forecast.
- **Ben Horowitz, *The Hard Thing About Hard Things* (Harper
  Business, 2014).** Operator perspective on the raise / cut / kill
  decisions on the runway curve — the reasoning inside a real CEO's
  head when the numbers get bad. Reads with
  [chapter 06](./06-decision-months-on-the-runway-curve.md).
- **Karen Berman & Joe Knight, *Financial Intelligence for
  Entrepreneurs* (Harvard Business Review Press, 2008).** The
  founder-friendly bridge from "cash-forward operating model" to
  proper accrual-based financial statements — useful before you hire
  a first CFO. Relevant to the boundary in
  [chapter 07](./07-boundary-startup-finance-fundraising.md).

## Employer-cost / fully-loaded references

- **US Bureau of Labor Statistics, *Employer Costs for Employee
  Compensation (ECEC)* quarterly release.**
  [bls.gov/ncs/ect](https://www.bls.gov/ncs/ect/). The authoritative
  US data on wages plus benefits as a percentage of total
  compensation. The empirical basis for the "1.25–1.4× fully-loaded"
  rule of thumb in [chapter 03](./03-fully-loaded-hire-cost.md).
- **Society for Human Resource Management (SHRM), *Cost-per-Hire
  Standard* (ANSI/SHRM-06001-2019).** The industry standard for
  computing recruiting cost as a component of fully-loaded hire cost.
  [shrm.org](https://www.shrm.org/).
  <!-- needs-research: stable URL for the ANSI/SHRM-06001-2019 standard document. -->
- **MIT Sloan / SBA guidance on true cost of an employee.** Commonly
  cited variations of the "1.25–1.4× base salary" rule; useful for
  cross-checking the specific loading you're computing for your own
  team.

## Notes on citation hygiene

Where a specific claim in this module cites a fact that would
benefit from a primary source and I could not verify one at
authoring time, the chapter has an inline `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`
comment marking the gap. These do not block reading; they mark
items for later verification and block the automated merge
pipeline until resolved. If you spot a citation that should be
present or a source that has been superseded, opening an issue
against this repository is the fastest way to fix it.
