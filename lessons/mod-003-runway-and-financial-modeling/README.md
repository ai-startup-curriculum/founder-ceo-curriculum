# Module 003 — Runway & Financial Modeling

> **Stage:** SEED · **Pillar:** Finance · **Prereq:** mod-002

Your canvas and unit economics said the business *could* work. This
module is where you find out how long you have to prove it. Every
downstream decision — who to hire, when to raise, what to cut, whether
to keep going — reduces to three numbers: **cash on hand, monthly net
burn, and the runway that falls out of the two**. A founder who can't
state those from memory is flying blind.

The most expensive mistake at this stage is *discovering* your runway
instead of *deciding* it. Founders who don't model their spend end up
on a schedule set by their bank balance: hiring in the good months,
panic-cutting when the number gets scary, starting to raise the month
they realise they should have started six months ago. A model is how
you get in front of the calendar instead of chasing it.

The operating plan is not a forecast — it will be wrong. Its job is to
make the **cost of being wrong visible in advance**, so that when
reality diverges from the plan (it will), you already know which lever
to pull. Deep FP&A, three-statement modeling, and financial-controls
craft as a permanent CFO discipline is deferred to the
`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` peer track (see
[chapter 07](./07-boundary-startup-finance-fundraising.md)).

## Learning objectives

After this module you can:

1. Compute and state, from memory, the three founder numbers — **cash
   on hand, monthly net burn, runway** — for a given month, and explain
   the difference between **gross** and **net** burn.
2. Recognise the "we'll make it up in revenue" trap and **stress-test
   runway against gross burn** as if customer revenue disappeared for a
   quarter. Report both numbers in board updates and internal reviews.
3. Model the **fully-loaded cost of a hire** (rough US-market
   `base × 1.25–1.4×`), plus **ramp lag** (6–12 weeks to start, another
   quarter or two to productivity) and **knock-on costs** (management
   time, tooling, HR/finance step-functions).
4. Build an **18-month operating model** with three sections — cash in,
   cash out, cash balance — every input a named assumption, every hire
   on the calendar, and the **zero-cash date** highlighted.
5. Apply Paul Graham's **default-alive vs default-dead** test
   (*Default Alive or Default Dead?*, October 2015) to decide whether
   the next move is to raise, cut, or grow into profitability.
6. Name the **decision months** on the runway curve — the "raise by,"
   the "cut by," and the "kill by" months — and what evidence would
   change each call.
7. Locate the **boundary to `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`**
   (level 30) — this module owns the founder-scale operating model, the
   peer track owns the CFO-scale FP&A and financial-controls discipline.

end-to-end, then stress-test it, then drill the two decisions

survives contact with reality.

## Live-lab note

If you're an operating founder using this as a live lab: build the
model on **your** current bank balance, **your** current payroll, and
**your** actual hiring pipeline. The scenario numbers in the exercises
are calibrated to a realistic pre-seed situation, but the point of the
work is a plan for a real business — yours or the simulated one — not
an academic sheet.
