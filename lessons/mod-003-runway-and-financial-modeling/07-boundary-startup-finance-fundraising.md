# Chapter 07 — Boundary: This Module Owns the Founder-Scale Operating Model; the Startup-Finance-Fundraising Track Owns the CFO-Scale Discipline

> **Reads with:** module objective 7 — *locate the boundary to
> `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` (level 30) — this module
> owns the founder-scale operating model; the peer track owns the
> CFO-scale FP&A and financial-controls discipline.*

## Why the boundary exists

Founder/CEO curriculum modules are role pathways over shared
functional curricula, not a separate body of knowledge (see the
top-level `README.md` and `PREREQUISITES.md`). The founder learns
*enough of every function to run the company*; the deeper craft of
each function is owned by the pathway for the role that will
eventually do it full-time. Financial modeling and cash management
follow the same rule.

- **This module (`founder-ceo/mod-003-runway-and-financial-modeling`)**
  teaches you to build and run the *founder-scale operating model* —
  the cash-forward, month-by-month spreadsheet a seed-stage founder
  personally owns and updates. You leave this module able to state
  the three founder numbers from memory, distinguish gross from net
  burn, model a hire honestly, build an 18-month operating plan, and
  name the raise-by / cut-by / kill-by decision months on the runway
  curve.
- **The startup-finance-fundraising track
  (`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`, level 30)** teaches the
  deeper craft of financial planning and analysis, three-statement
  modeling, financial controls, and audit-ready accounting as a
  **permanent CFO discipline** — the work a first Head of Finance,
  Controller, or CFO does once the company is large enough to need
  formal financial statements, real internal controls, and
  investor-grade reporting.

If you are the founder-CEO of a pre-finance-hire company, both are
your job today (with a fractional CFO or a bookkeeper filling
whatever slice you can't reach yourself). Once a finance leader is in
seat, the deeper FP&A and controls work transfers to them; this
module's material becomes what you use to **supervise and
partner** with the finance function, not to **practice at depth**.

## What "the founder-scale operating model" covers, and what it deliberately doesn't

This module is scoped to the founder artifact that carries every
seed-stage financial decision: the 18-month, cash-forward operating
model plus the discipline of running the runway calendar off it.

Explicitly **in** scope for this module:

- The **three founder numbers** — cash, net burn, runway — stated
  from memory ([chapter 01](./01-three-founder-numbers.md)).
- The **gross vs net burn** distinction and the one-quarter
  revenue-disappearance stress test
  ([chapter 02](./02-gross-vs-net-burn-trap.md)).
- **Fully-loaded hire cost, ramp lag, and knock-on costs** as inputs
  to the model ([chapter 03](./03-fully-loaded-hire-cost.md)).
- The **18-month operating model** itself — three sections, named
  assumptions, hires on the calendar, zero-cash date
  ([chapter 04](./04-eighteen-month-operating-model.md)).
- The **default-alive/dead diagnostic**
  ([chapter 05](./05-default-alive-vs-default-dead.md)) and the
  **raise-by / cut-by / kill-by decision months**
  ([chapter 06](./06-decision-months-on-the-runway-curve.md)).

Explicitly **out** of scope for this module — deferred to the
startup-finance-fundraising track:

- **Three-statement modeling at depth.** A full linked P&L, balance
  sheet, and cash-flow statement with proper accrual accounting,
  working-capital modeling, deferred revenue, and tax provisions.
  The founder-scale operating model is cash-forward only; the CFO's
  three-statement model integrates accruals and reconciles to the
  audited financials.
- **GAAP revenue recognition.** The founder model treats "cash
  received this month" as revenue for burn purposes; ASC 606 /
  IFRS 15 compliant revenue recognition (recognising over
  contractual periods, splitting distinct performance obligations,
  handling variable consideration) is the finance function's craft.
  <!-- needs-research: primary-source references for ASC 606 (FASB) and IFRS 15 (IASB) revenue-recognition standards. -->
- **Financial controls and audit readiness.** Segregation of duties,
  approval matrices, month-end close cadence, journal-entry review,
  the systems and processes that support an audit or a due-diligence
  data room. Founder-run companies typically don't have these; a
  Series-A / Series-B company will need them.
- **Full FP&A discipline.** Budget-vs-actuals reporting, department-
  level P&Ls, variance analysis, driver-based forecasting at scale,
  scenario-planning frameworks that handle dozens of interacting
  levers. The 18-month operating model in this course is the
  founder's tool; the FP&A machine that supports a 100-person
  company is the finance function's.
- **Treasury and cash management.** Sweep accounts, money-market
  vs Treasury Bill vs CD laddering, banking-relationship management,
  FX hedging for international operations, corporate credit-card
  programmes and expense-management systems.
- **Tax strategy and structure.** R&D tax credits (US Section 41),
  §174 capitalisation, state nexus, transfer pricing for
  international subsidiaries, sales-tax registration and
  compliance, deferred tax accounting.
  <!-- needs-research: primary-source references for IRC §41 (R&D tax credit) and §174 (research & experimental expenditures capitalisation post-TCJA). -->
- **Equity accounting and stock-based compensation.** ASC 718
  expense modeling, 409A valuations at scale, ESPP mechanics if
  offered, secondary transactions. Basic cap-table hygiene is
  covered in mod-005; the accounting mechanics live in the finance
  track.
- **Audit management.** Preparing for and managing an annual audit,
  responding to auditor questions, remediating findings.
- **Investor-grade reporting and covenants.** Board-package
  financials to GAAP, lender covenants and monitoring, revenue-based
  financing structures.

The distinction isn't sharp on any single question — a founder
building the 18-month model touches, in miniature, most of what a
CFO does at scale — but it is sharp enough at the *rigor, cadence,
and organisational integration* level to justify a handoff. One
cash-forward operating model that the founder personally owns is
seed-stage work; the audited financials, formal controls, and
scaled FP&A are the finance function's.

## When to graduate from this module to the finance track

Three signals it's time to move financial-modeling work from
"founder does it in a spreadsheet at 11pm" to "finance function owns
it":

- **First real finance hire in seat.** A first Head of Finance,
  Controller, or CFO changes what the founder should own. Hand off
  the operating model, the assumption block, the monthly ritual,
  and the raise/cut/kill calendar as the starting corpus and the
  record of prior decisions. The finance leader will rebuild it in
  their own tooling — that's fine and expected.
- **The company needs GAAP financials on a schedule.** Priced
  Series-A rounds, venture debt, larger enterprise customers (SOC 2
  / ISO 27001 processes), and eventually IPO readiness all require
  monthly close and audited or reviewed financials. Founder
  spreadsheets don't produce those; a finance function does.
- **The number of levers exceeds what a spreadsheet the founder owns
  can honestly model.** At 30–60 people, the operating model has
  enough moving parts — departmental budgets, hiring plans across
  functions, per-product P&Ls, per-customer margin analysis — that
  a purpose-built FP&A tool (or a properly-engineered model owned
  by finance) is the honest answer, not a bigger version of the
  founder's sheet.

Before those signals, this module's material is sufficient. After
them, this module's material remains true — the three founder
numbers, the gross-vs-net discipline, the default-alive test, the
raise/cut/kill calendar don't stop being right — but the operating
pattern they live inside is different, and that is what the finance
track teaches.

## What the founder-CEO still owes the finance function, always

The boundary transfers the *craft* to the finance track. It does not
transfer the *judgment* — that is permanently CEO work. The
founder-CEO's ongoing model-owner obligations, at every stage of the
company, are:

- **Own the three founder numbers.** No matter how large the finance
  function grows, the CEO who cannot state cash, net burn, and
  runway from memory has outsourced something that will be asked in
  every board meeting and fundraising conversation for the life of
  the company.
- **Own the default-alive/dead call.** The finance function models
  the numbers; the CEO decides what the diagnosis means for
  strategy. That decision is fed by finance data but not delegated
  to it.
- **Own the raise / cut / kill decisions.** Finance can name the
  dates on the calendar; the CEO makes the calls. In particular
  the "kill by" decision is one no CFO should make alone.
- **Refuse to let the numbers become fiction.** A finance function
  producing beautiful reports that the CEO doesn't personally
  understand is a dangerous condition. Periodic re-derivation of
  the top-line numbers from first principles — "walk me through how
  net burn moved from last month to this month" — is a permanent
  founder responsibility.
- **Own the cash-forward horizon in the head.** Even with a full
  finance team, the founder should carry the zero-cash date and the
  three decision months in the front of their mind at all times.
  Anything else delegates the most consequential number in the
  company.

The material in this module is the founder-CEO's baseline
financial-modeling literacy. It is designed to be re-run whenever a
strategic decision changes the shape of the burn: a new hiring plan,
a market pivot, a new product line, a big contract win or loss, a
raise. Every one of those deserves the same 18-month-model
discipline the original one got.

## Summary

- This module ends at the **founder-scale operating model** and the
  **raise/cut/kill decision calendar**. You leave it able to build
  the model, run the monthly ritual, and defend the diagnosis.
- The **deep FP&A, three-statement modeling, financial controls,
  audit readiness, treasury, and tax craft** is owned by the
  **`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` (level 30)** peer
  track. Defer to it once a finance function exists.
- Transfer *craft* to the finance track; keep *judgment* (the three
  numbers, the default-alive call, the raise/cut/kill decisions,
  personal ownership of the runway calendar) permanently with the
  CEO.
- Re-run this module's method on every strategic inflection. The
  operating model doesn't get stale; your certainty about the
  current plan does.
