# Chapter 07 — Boundary: This Module Owns the First Canvas; the Startup-Product-GTM Track Owns the Discipline

> **Reads with:** module objective 7 — *cite the boundary to
> `startup-product-gtm-curriculum` (level 30) for deep pricing / packaging
> / channel-economics craft — this module owns the first canvas, the peer
> track owns the discipline.*

## Why the boundary exists

Founder/CEO curriculum modules are role pathways over shared functional
curricula, not a separate body of knowledge (see `README.md` and
`PREREQUISITES.md`). The founder learns *enough of every function to
run the company*; the deeper craft of each function is owned by the
pathway for the role that will eventually do it full-time. Business
modeling and unit economics follow the same rule.

- **This module (`founder-ceo/mod-002-lean-business-modeling`)** teaches
  you to build the *first* lean canvas and the *first* unit-economics
  sheet from your mod-001 discovery findings — the founder artifacts
  that carry every downstream financial and fundraising decision. You
  leave this module able to fill the nine blocks problem-first, name
  and defend the four core numbers, derive the three ratios, name the
  single riskiest assumption, and size the market bottom-up as a
  sanity check.
- **The startup-product-gtm track (`startup-product-gtm-curriculum`,
  level 30)** teaches the deeper craft of pricing, packaging, and
  channel economics as a **permanent go-to-market discipline** — the
  work a Head of Product or Head of Growth does once the company has
  a shipping product, a working sales motion, and enough data to
  reason about tiers, discount ladders, per-channel CAC, and cohort
  segmentation with statistical rigor.

If you are the founder-CEO of a pre-GTM-hire company, both are your
job today, and you should be aware of both. Once a product / growth
hire is in seat, the sustained pricing-and-channel discipline
transfers to them; this module's material becomes what you use to
**supervise**, not to **practice at depth**.

## What "the first canvas" covers, and what it deliberately doesn't

This module is scoped to a one-week (~6–8 focused-hour) analysis
that produces three founder artifacts — a lean canvas, a
unit-economics sheet, and a riskiest-assumption call — from your
mod-001 discovery evidence.

Explicitly **in** scope for this module:

- Filling the lean canvas problem-first from discovery evidence
  ([chapter 01](./01-lean-canvas-problem-first.md)).
- Choosing between the Lean Canvas and the Business Model Canvas
  ([chapter 02](./02-lean-vs-business-model-canvas.md)).
- Building the four-number unit-economics sheet with the founder-time
  trap called out ([chapter 03](./03-unit-economics-four-numbers.md)).
- Deriving LTV, LTV:CAC, and CAC payback as heuristics, not laws
  ([chapter 04](./04-ltv-cac-payback-heuristics.md)).
- Naming the single riskiest assumption and designing the cheapest
  experiment ([chapter 05](./05-riskiest-assumption-experiment.md)).
- Bottom-up market sizing as a sanity check
  ([chapter 06](./06-bottom-up-market-sizing.md)).

Explicitly **out** of scope for this module — deferred to the
startup-product-gtm track:

- **Pricing research and design at depth.** Van Westendorp Price
  Sensitivity Meter, conjoint analysis, willingness-to-pay research
  methodology, discount-ladder design, packaging strategy across
  tiers, value-metric selection, and the operational cadence of
  quarterly pricing reviews.
  <!-- needs-research: primary-source citation for Van Westendorp PSM — Peter H. van Westendorp, "NSS Price Sensitivity Meter (PSM) — a new approach to consumer perception of prices," ESOMAR 1976. -->
- **Per-channel CAC modeling and channel-portfolio design.** How to
  model paid vs organic vs partnership economics separately, how to
  measure channel-specific payback, how to balance a portfolio
  across channel maturity, and the operational discipline of
  quarterly channel review.
- **Cohort segmentation and retention analysis at scale.** Cohort
  curves per segment, per acquisition channel, per pricing tier;
  survival analysis and its right and wrong applications; the
  operational cadence of retention review.
- **Sales-motion selection and sales-economics modeling.** PLG vs
  sales-led vs hybrid; the economics of each motion; the trigger
  points for evolving from founder-led to first sales hire to
  segmented AE structure (this specifically overlaps with mod-006
  Founder-led Sales in scope, but the *repeated-play* discipline is
  a GTM-track topic).
- **Expansion revenue design.** Net revenue retention as a
  first-class metric; land-and-expand product design; upsell
  triggers and their measurement; the specific economics of
  seat-based vs usage-based vs feature-tier expansion.
- **Marketing-mix modeling and attribution.** Multi-touch
  attribution, incrementality testing, the shift from
  last-click to media-mix modeling as the company matures.

The distinction isn't sharp on any single question — a founder
building the first canvas touches, in miniature, most of what a
GTM-function owner does at scale — but it is sharp enough at the
*cadence, rigor, and organisational integration* level to justify a
handoff. One canvas and one unit-economics sheet is the founder's
job; the canvas becoming a live GTM operating model reviewed
quarterly is the GTM function's.

## When to graduate from this module to the GTM track

Three signals it's time to move pricing-and-channel work from
"founder does it between everything else" to "GTM function owns it":

- **GTM hire in seat.** As soon as you have a first Head of Growth,
  Head of Product, or VP Sales, the deep pricing / packaging /
  channel work is theirs to build. Hand off the artifacts (this
  module's canvas and unit-economics sheet, plus mod-003's
  operating model) as the starting corpus and the record of prior
  decisions.
- **The product has enough live data to argue with.** Once there
  are hundreds to thousands of paying customers, cohort curves per
  segment are honest, per-channel CAC is measurable rather than
  modeled, and the GTM track's statistical methods start to earn
  their keep. Before this point, the founder-level heuristics from
  this module are the right tool.
- **The pattern of "one canvas per year" is failing.** If the
  canvas has become an annual planning artifact and the market
  has moved twice inside a year, you need continuous model
  re-examination — a GTM-track topic — not a bigger annual canvas.

Before those signals, this module's material is sufficient. After
them, this module's material remains true — the Lean Canvas
discipline, the four core numbers, and the riskiest-assumption
principle never stop being right — but the operating pattern they
live inside is different, and that is what the GTM track teaches.

## What this module's founder still owes the model, always

The boundary transfers the *craft* to the GTM track. It does not
transfer the *judgment* — that is permanently CEO work. The
founder-CEO's ongoing model-owner obligations, at every stage of
the company, are:

- **Own the unit-economics narrative to the board and investors.**
  A CEO who cannot fluently defend the four core numbers and the
  three derived metrics on their own company's sheet has outsourced
  something that will be asked about in every fundraising and every
  board meeting for the life of the company.
- **Own the model's riskiest-assumption call at every strategic
  inflection.** New market, new segment, new pricing tier, new
  channel bet — each of these creates a new load-bearing
  assumption, and naming it is CEO work even after a GTM function
  is staffed. The chapter 05 discipline is a permanent muscle.
- **Own the segment framing.** The GTM function optimises inside a
  segment; the CEO decides *which* segment the company competes
  for. That decision is fed by GTM data but not delegated to it.
- **Refuse to let the model become fiction.** A canvas that hasn't
  been updated in a year, or a unit-economics sheet that no longer
  reflects the current pricing or cost structure, is worse than no
  model — it produces confident wrong answers. The CEO's
  discipline is to force periodic honest re-examination even when
  the operating team is happy with the status quo.

The material in this module is the founder-CEO's baseline
business-modeling literacy. It is designed to be re-run whenever a
strategic decision changes the shape of the bet: a new segment, a
new product line, an acquired company, a pricing overhaul, a
platform shift. Every one of those deserves the same first-canvas
discipline the original one got.

## Summary

- This module ends at the **first canvas** and the **first
  unit-economics sheet**. You leave it able to build both
  end-to-end and defend them against a skeptical read.
- The **deep pricing / packaging / channel-economics craft** —
  van Westendorp, conjoint, cohort segmentation, per-channel CAC
  modeling, sales-motion economics, expansion-revenue design,
  attribution — is owned by the **`startup-product-gtm-curriculum`
  (level 30)** peer track. Defer to it once a GTM function exists.
- Transfer *craft* to the GTM track; keep *judgment* (segment,
  strategy, personal ownership of the unit-economics narrative,
  the riskiest-assumption call) permanently with the CEO.
- Re-run this module's method on every strategic inflection. The
  Lean Canvas doesn't get stale; your certainty about the current
  model does.
