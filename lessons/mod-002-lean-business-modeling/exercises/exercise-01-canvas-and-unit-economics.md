# Exercise 01 — Lean Canvas & Unit Economics

**Module:** 002 Lean Business Modeling · **Stage:** IDEA→PRE-SEED · **Time:**
~6–8 focused hours (one working week for an operating founder) · **Reads
with:** chapters [01](../01-lean-canvas-problem-first.md),
[02](../02-lean-vs-business-model-canvas.md),
[03](../03-unit-economics-four-numbers.md), and
[04](../04-ltv-cac-payback-heuristics.md).

## Problem statement

You have discovery evidence that a problem is real (from mod-001) and no
business model yet — only a private belief about who this is for and how
the company might make money. This exercise turns those beliefs into two
one-page founder artifacts that can be defended to a skeptical
co-founder or investor: a **lean canvas** filled problem-first from
discovery evidence, and a **unit-economics sheet** whose four core
numbers and three derived metrics are traceable to sources or explicitly
marked as assumptions.

The exercise is not to produce the "right" business model. It is to
produce a model **specific enough to be wrong**, so that the next
exercise can attack its riskiest assumption instead of drifting through
generalities.

## Deliverables (two founder artifacts)

1. **A lean canvas** — one page, nine blocks, filled in the Ash Maurya
   recommended order (Segment → Problem → UVP → Solution → Channels →
   Revenue → Cost → Key Metrics → Unfair Advantage). Every block carries
   a one-line **evidence note** citing the discovery interview,
   prior-spend signal, competitor teardown, or public data point that
   supports it — or the explicit label **ASSUMPTION**.
2. **A unit-economics sheet** — one page, six inputs (ARPA, variable
   cost per account, gross margin %, monthly churn, average customer
   lifetime, CAC) plus three derived metrics (LTV, LTV:CAC, CAC
   payback). Each input cites its source or is marked **ASSUMPTION**.
   CAC includes founder time at a fair-market replacement rate per
   [chapter 03](../03-unit-economics-four-numbers.md).

## Scenario

Pick one:

- **Your real startup** (recommended if you're an operating founder):
  use your actual mod-001 discovery notes and your real (or best-guess)
  numbers.
- **Simulated:** use the mod-001 exemplar's segment — *first-time
  technical founders in their first 12 months of raising* — and the
  sharpened problem framing ("what to learn when; what to ignore now").
  Assume a subscription product priced somewhere in the $20–$200/mo
  range; you choose the point and defend it with a plausible source
  (competitor pricing, prior-spend signal, willingness-to-pay evidence).

## Requirements

The two artifacts have to satisfy each of the following, checkably, by
the end of the exercise:

### Canvas

- **Filled problem-first**, not left-to-right. The evidence for the
  filling order is that upstream blocks (Segment, Problem) do not depend
  on downstream ones (Cost, Metrics), while every downstream block
  depends on the upstream ones.
- **Segment is one recognisable sentence, with an early-adopter
  carve-out.** Not "SMBs" or "developers." A filter you could apply to
  LinkedIn and get a coherent list of names back.
- **Problem block cites 1–3 problems from discovery**, in the buyer's
  language, with **existing alternatives** named (a specific
  competitor, a specific spreadsheet workflow, a specific manual
  workaround, or "nothing").
- **UVP is one sentence**, outcome-focused, aimed at the segment. A
  "high-concept pitch" line ("X for Y") is optional but useful.
- **Solution has one line per problem**, mapped 1:1 to the Problem
  block.
- **Channels names free/owned, paid, and founder-driven** channels
  separately. Each named channel has a plausible cost-to-reach that
  you could test.
- **Revenue block has a specific price point** (or a defended range)
  and a **willingness-to-pay signal** — competitor pricing, discovery
  prior-spend answer, or a comparable pricing source. "TBD" is not a
  legitimate answer.
- **Cost block distinguishes fixed from variable per-account** costs.
  For AI-heavy products, per-request inference cost is a required
  line item.
- **Key Metrics lists 3–5 numbers** that would move first if the model
  were right (not the ones that would move eventually).
- **Unfair Advantage is honest.** An empty box is a legitimate answer;
  "we work harder" or "our team is great" is not.
- **Every block carries a one-line evidence note** — either citing a
  source, or explicitly labelled **ASSUMPTION**. Not conflating the two
  is the whole point.

### Unit-economics sheet

- **Six inputs, each with a source or the label ASSUMPTION.** ARPA,
  variable cost per account per period, gross margin %, monthly churn
  rate, average customer lifetime, CAC.
- **CAC includes founder time** valued at a fair-market replacement
  rate (the salary you'd pay to hire the person who would eventually
  take over the demo calls, outbound sequences, and content posts).
  A CAC computed with founder-time-at-zero fails this criterion.
- **Gross margin is a real percentage**, not left blank. If you don't
  yet know per-account variable cost, use a defensible estimate and
  mark it **ASSUMPTION** — but a number must appear.
- **LTV formula includes the gross-margin term**, not just ARPA ×
  lifetime. Revenue-based LTV is disqualified.
- **LTV:CAC ratio is reported** as a number, and compared to the >3×
  SaaS heuristic.
- **CAC payback is reported in months**, and compared to the <~12
  months SaaS heuristic.
- **Churn is stated with its sample size and window.** "Our churn is
  2%" on 15 customers over 3 months is directional, not a metric; say
  so.
- **One-line diagnosis** at the bottom: which single input, if halved,
  breaks the model?

## Starter guidance

**Session 1 (~2 hr) — canvas draft.** Read chapters
[01](../01-lean-canvas-problem-first.md) and
[02](../02-lean-vs-business-model-canvas.md). Print or lay out the
nine-block template. Fill in the Maurya order (Segment → Problem → UVP
→ Solution → Channels → Revenue → Cost → Key Metrics → Unfair
Advantage), one block at a time, each in one to three lines. Resist the
temptation to jump to Solution or Revenue first — the discipline is
what produces the artifact.

**Session 2 (~1 hr) — evidence notes.** For every block, add the one-line
evidence note or the **ASSUMPTION** label. Count the assumptions. If more
than seven blocks are ASSUMPTION, you have a model built primarily on
opinion — that's information, not failure, but you should know it before
exercise 02.

**Session 3 (~2 hr) — unit-economics sheet.** Read chapters
[03](../03-unit-economics-four-numbers.md) and
[04](../04-ltv-cac-payback-heuristics.md). Fill the six inputs; compute
the three derived metrics. Include founder time in CAC at a fair-market
replacement rate. State the sample size and window for churn.

**Session 4 (~1–2 hr) — skeptic pass.** Show both artifacts to a
co-founder or peer. The pass is done when they cannot point to a block
or a number and ask "where does this come from?" without you having a
one-line answer — evidence or ASSUMPTION.

## Acceptance criteria

You are done — and this exercise has met the module's standard — when
*all* of the following are true:

- [ ] The canvas has **all nine blocks filled**, in one to three lines
      each, on one page.
- [ ] Every block carries a **one-line evidence note or the ASSUMPTION
      label**. You can count how many of each.
- [ ] The **segment is one recognisable sentence** with an
      early-adopter carve-out.
- [ ] The **problem block cites 1–3 problems from discovery** with
      **existing alternatives** named.
- [ ] The **revenue block has a specific price** with a
      **willingness-to-pay signal** cited.
- [ ] The **cost block distinguishes fixed from variable per-account**
      costs; per-request inference cost is a line item for
      AI-heavy products.
- [ ] The unit-economics sheet has **all six inputs filled**, each with
      source or the ASSUMPTION label.
- [ ] **CAC includes founder time** at a stated hourly rate.
- [ ] **LTV includes the gross-margin term** (revenue-based LTV is
      disqualified).
- [ ] **LTV:CAC and CAC payback** are reported and compared to the
      SaaS heuristics — with a one-line explanation if the heuristic
      does not apply (marketplace, consumer, enterprise multi-year,
      pre-data).
- [ ] The sheet ends with a **one-line diagnosis** naming the single
      input that, if halved, would break the model.

## Self-assessment rubric

| Dimension | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Segment | "SMBs" / "developers" | Narrow early adopter, recognisable in one sentence |
| Problem block | Restated solution ("they need our tool") | Ripped from discovery, existing alternatives named |
| UVP | Feature list | One-sentence, outcome-focused, aimed at the segment |
| Channels | "SEO + content" hand-wave | Named channels with testable cost-to-reach |
| Revenue | "We'll figure out pricing later" | A price with willingness-to-pay evidence |
| Evidence notes | Missing on most blocks | Present on every block; assumptions labelled |
| CAC | Founder-time-at-zero | Founder time at fair-market replacement rate |
| Gross margin | Absent or "TBD" | Real percentage with variable-cost breakdown |
| LTV | Revenue × lifetime | ARPA × gross margin × lifetime, formula shown |
| Heuristic check | Number reported without context | LTV:CAC and payback vs SaaS heuristics, with N/A explained where they don't apply |
| Diagnosis | Absent | One-line naming of the input that, if halved, breaks the model |

## Definition of done

A skeptical seed-stage investor or co-founder reading your canvas and
economics sheet would agree that (a) the biggest bet in the business is
the one your diagnosis line points at, (b) the specific inputs supporting
that diagnosis are traceable to real evidence where possible and marked
as assumptions where not, and (c) the model as a whole is *specific
enough to be wrong* — which is the prerequisite for exercise 02, where
you'll design the cheapest experiment to test whether it is.
