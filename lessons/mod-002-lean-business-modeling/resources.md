# Resources — Module 002 Lean Business Modeling & Unit Economics

Real, citable references for the material in this module. Primary
sources are listed first per topic; secondary sources for narrower
application (worked examples, community write-ups) follow.

## Books (primary references)

- **Ash Maurya, *Running Lean: Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That
  Works* (O'Reilly; 3rd ed., 2022).** The canonical lean-canvas book
  and source of the nine-block-adaptation used in
  [chapter 01](./01-lean-canvas-problem-first.md) and the
  problem-first fill order, plus the riskiest-assumption discipline in
  [chapter 05](./05-riskiest-assumption-experiment.md). Author's site:
  [leanstack.com](https://leanstack.com/).
  <!-- needs-research: ISBN and stable URL for Running Lean, 3rd edition, O'Reilly, 2022. -->
- **Alexander Osterwalder & Yves Pigneur, *Business Model Generation:
  A Handbook for Visionaries, Game Changers, and Challengers* (Wiley,
  2010).** The original Business Model Canvas — the reference
  distinguished from the Lean Canvas in
  [chapter 02](./02-lean-vs-business-model-canvas.md). Author's site:
  [strategyzer.com](https://www.strategyzer.com/).
  <!-- needs-research: ISBN and stable URL. -->
- **Eric Ries, *The Lean Startup: How Today's Entrepreneurs Use
  Continuous Innovation to Create Radically Successful Businesses*
  (Crown Business, 2011).** The validated-learning framing that
  underpins [chapter 05](./05-riskiest-assumption-experiment.md), and
  the build-measure-learn loop that assumption-testing instantiates.
- **Steve Blank & Bob Dorf, *The Startup Owner's Manual: The Step-by-
  Step Guide for Building a Great Company* (K&S Ranch, 2012).** The
  operationalised customer-development manual; the discovery /
  validation distinction referenced across mod-001 and mod-002.
- **Steve Blank, *The Four Steps to the Epiphany: Successful Strategies
  for Products that Win* (2005; revised editions since).** The origin
  of the customer-development model and its market-size-honesty
  discipline referenced in [chapter 06](./06-bottom-up-market-sizing.md).
- **Bill Aulet, *Disciplined Entrepreneurship: 24 Steps to a
  Successful Startup* (Wiley, 2013).** MIT's step-by-step framework;
  strong on bottom-up market sizing (steps 3–5) and unit-economics
  fundamentals (steps 15–17). Complementary structured alternative to
  the lean-canvas route.

## Unit-economics primary references

- **David Skok, *For Entrepreneurs* blog
  ([forentrepreneurs.com](https://www.forentrepreneurs.com/)).** The
  canonical online reference for SaaS unit economics: "SaaS Metrics
  2.0," "The SaaS P&L Explained," "The Startup's Unit Economics," and
  the LTV : CAC > 3× / payback < 12-month heuristics referenced in
  [chapter 04](./04-ltv-cac-payback-heuristics.md).
  <!-- needs-research: stable URL and publication year for the specific "SaaS Metrics 2.0" post citing the >3× LTV:CAC heuristic. -->
- **Bill Gurley, "All Revenue is Not Created Equal: The Keys to the
  10X Revenue Club" (Above the Crowd, 2011).** Argues that revenue
  quality (margin, retention, defensibility) matters as much as
  revenue size — the intellectual foundation of the gross-margin
  emphasis in [chapter 03](./03-unit-economics-four-numbers.md).
  <!-- needs-research: stable URL — commonly hosted at abovethecrowd.com. -->
- **Bessemer Venture Partners, "The State of the Cloud" annual reports
  and "The Bessemer 10 Laws of Cloud."** Long-standing published
  benchmarks for SaaS unit economics, including CAC payback, net
  revenue retention, and gross margin ranges.
  [bvp.com/atlas/state-of-the-cloud](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/state-of-the-cloud).
  <!-- needs-research: verify current URL and title; Bessemer content has been reorganised over time. -->
- **a16z (Andreessen Horowitz) enterprise / SaaS content.**
  ([a16z.com](https://a16z.com/)). Regular essays on SaaS metrics,
  usage-based pricing, and the shift from perpetual-license to
  subscription to consumption models. Look for essays by Martin
  Casado, Sarah Wang, and Kristina Shen.
- **KeyBanc Capital Markets / SaaS Capital / OpenView SaaS
  Benchmarks.** Annual industry-wide reports on gross margin, CAC
  payback, and growth rates by ARR bucket. The best empirical anchors
  for the SaaS heuristic ranges used in
  [chapter 04](./04-ltv-cac-payback-heuristics.md).
  <!-- needs-research: current stable URLs for KeyBanc SaaS survey, SaaS Capital reports, and OpenView Benchmarks 2024/2025 editions. -->

## Pricing and go-to-market references (peer track boundary)

Cited for the boundary in [chapter 07](./07-boundary-gtm-pricing-channels.md);
these are the primary references the deeper craft in
`startup-product-gtm-curriculum` draws on.

- **Peter H. van Westendorp, "NSS Price Sensitivity Meter (PSM) — a
  new approach to consumer perception of prices" (ESOMAR, 1976).**
  The canonical willingness-to-pay research technique; deep pricing
  research method in the peer track.
  <!-- needs-research: stable ESOMAR reference / DOI. -->
- **Madhavan Ramanujam & Georg Tacke, *Monetizing Innovation: How
  Smart Companies Design the Product Around the Price* (Wiley,
  2016).** Simon-Kucher's methodology for pricing new products
  through customer research before build.
- **Patrick Campbell / ProfitWell / Paddle content
  ([paddle.com/blog](https://www.paddle.com/blog)).** Long-running
  practitioner writing on SaaS pricing, packaging, and cohort
  retention.
- **Kyle Poyar, "Growth Unhinged"
  ([growthunhinged.com](https://www.growthunhinged.com/)).** Regular
  benchmark posts on pricing tier structure, freemium conversion, and
  usage-based pricing economics.

## Market-sizing primary references

  3–5.** The clearest step-by-step methodology for bottom-up market
  sizing, including primary market research, end-user profile, and
  TAM calculation. Referenced in
  [chapter 06](./06-bottom-up-market-sizing.md).
- **Michael Skok, *Underscore VC* essays on market sizing.**
  [underscore.vc](https://www.underscore.vc/) — practitioner-facing
  essays on TAM/SAM/SOM defensibility for investor-facing pitches.

## Notes on citation hygiene

Where a specific claim in this module cites a fact that would benefit
from a primary source and I could not verify one at authoring time,
the chapter has an inline `<!-- needs-research: ... -->` comment
marking the gap. These do not block reading; they mark items for
later verification and block the automated merge pipeline until
resolved. If you spot a citation that should be present or a source
that has been superseded, opening an issue against this repository
is the fastest way to fix it.
