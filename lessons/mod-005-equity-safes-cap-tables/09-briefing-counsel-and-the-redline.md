# Chapter 09 — Briefing Counsel: The Founder Authors the Cap-Table Walk and the Redline; Counsel Delivers the Legal Opinion

> **Reads with:** module objective 8 — *brief legal counsel with a
> defensible cap-table walk and term-sheet redline, and route the
> legal opinion to counsel — this module reads and authors the
> paper, counsel delivers opinion.*

## Two documents, two owners

A well-run seed round produces two founder-facing artifacts and
one counsel-facing deliverable:

- **Cap-table walk** (founder-authored). The share-by-share walk
  from today's FD table through the priced round to post-close,
  produced by the founder using the mechanics in chapters 01–07.
- **Term-sheet redline** (founder-authored). The specific
  redlines against the deviations from the defaults in chapter
  08 — participation, multiples above 1×, full-ratchet,
  narrow-based anti-dilution, non-standard pool asks, non-
  standard preference stack.
- **Legal opinion** (counsel-authored). The formal legal review
  of the term sheet, the round documents, the corporate
  structure, and the specific tax and securities-law
  consequences. This is what counsel delivers **on top of** the
  founder-authored artifacts, not instead of them.

The founder authors the arithmetic and the redline; **counsel
delivers the opinion.** Founders who outsource the arithmetic
lose the ability to defend their own round; counsel who is
handed a legible cap-table walk and a specific redline can
deliver a better opinion at lower cost than counsel who is
handed a term sheet and asked "is this fine?"

## Why the founder authors, not counsel

Three reasons the founder should not outsource the cap-table
walk and the redline:

- **Counsel can't defend a founder's business judgement.**
  Whether to accept a 15% pool because the round is otherwise
  worth taking, or to hold the line at 10% and risk the lead
  walking, is a founder decision. Counsel can tell you the
  15% is above market; counsel cannot tell you whether to
  sign anyway.
- **Counsel bills per hour.** Every ambiguity in the founder's
  ask becomes counsel time. A founder who hands over "here
  is the cap-table walk I've done, here are the four
  redlines I want, please review and confirm the language"
  gets a $3–5k bill; a founder who hands over a term sheet
  and asks "is this fine?" gets a $10–20k bill for counsel
  to build the walk and identify the redlines themselves.
  <!-- needs-research: primary-source citation for typical seed-round counsel fees; Kruze Consulting, Cooley GO, and Y Combinator's *A Guide to Seed Fundraising* publish rough ranges, but numbers vary widely by firm and region. -->
- **The judgement transfers, permanently.** The founder who
  never authored their own cap-table walk at seed will not
  understand the Series A cap-table walk when it lands, and
  will be at a permanent information disadvantage relative
  to counsel and to the board. This is the same reason
  mod-003 insists the founder owns the model rather than
  outsourcing it to a fractional CFO.

Counsel is a **service provider**, not a **decision-maker**.
Counsel's expertise is the paper and the legal exposure; the
founder's expertise is the business trade-off. Both are
necessary; neither substitutes for the other.

## The founder-authored artifacts, in detail

### 1. The cap-table walk

Produce a document — spreadsheet or Markdown table — with the
four snapshots from chapter 06's format:

- **Snapshot 0 — Current state.** FD table today: founders,
  every issued option grant, reserved pool, every SAFE and
  note in the stack section (amount, cap, form, fixed
  ownership at conversion). FD % sums to 100.0%.
- **Snapshot 1 — Pre-round state.** Same table on the eve
  of the priced round, if any updates have occurred since
  Snapshot 0.
- **Snapshot 2 — Pool refresh + SAFE conversion, pre-new-
  money.** FD table after the pool refresh and every SAFE
  conversion, before new-money shares are issued. FD % sums
  to 100.0%.
- **Snapshot 3 — Post-close.** FD table after new-money
  shares are issued. FD % sums to 100.0%.

Add a **"where the dilution went" narrative** with one
sentence per transition, identifying who was diluted by how
many percentage points and why (pool refresh, SAFE
conversion, new-money issuance).

Add a **founder ownership curve** — combined founders'
percentage across the four snapshots — so the round's story
is legible on one line.

### 2. The term-sheet redline

Read the term sheet against the defaults in chapter 08. For
every clause that deviates, produce a specific redline:

- The **current clause language** (verbatim from the term
  sheet).
- The **default position** (from chapter 08 or from *Venture
  Deals*).
- The **specific ask** — what you want the clause to say.
- The **reasoning** in one sentence — why this deviation is
  worth the negotiation credit to remove.

Do this for **at least** the liquidation-preference and
anti-dilution clauses. Extend to other clauses (board seat,
protective provisions, drag-along, information rights) as
each deviates from what you'd sign.

A well-formatted redline is a two-column document: current
clause on the left, proposed clause on the right, with a note
underneath. Counsel then confirms the exact wording of the
proposed clause; the founder's job is to identify what the
clause should *do*.

### 3. The "structural three" note

From chapter 07, name the three structural decisions in the
current round most affecting founder ownership at close —
pool size, SAFE stack, round size and pre-money together —
and quantify each with the percentage-point impact on the
founders. This is the memo the founder brings to the
lead-partner conversation; it is not for counsel per se, but
counsel benefits from knowing which structural asks are the
founder's priority.

## The counsel-authored deliverable

Once the founder has produced the three artifacts, hand them
to counsel with a specific ask. Something like:

> Here is the cap-table walk from founding through the
> proposed priced round (attached). Here is the term-sheet
> redline against the four clauses I want to renegotiate
> (attached). Please:
> 1. Confirm the cap-table walk is arithmetically correct and
>    consistent with the current corporate documents.
> 2. Draft the specific language for the four redlines,
>    consistent with the NVCA model and your firm's
>    house style.
> 3. Identify any additional legal or tax exposure I should be
>    aware of that isn't reflected in my structural asks —
>    particularly around securities-law compliance, Section
>    409A implications, and any state-specific corporate-
>    law issues.
> 4. Attend the next term-sheet negotiation call to confirm
>    the language once we agree on structure.

That is the founder / counsel handoff. Counsel confirms the
arithmetic (not authors it), drafts the specific language
(not the redline itself), and flags the legal exposure the
founder can't see (which is the actual counsel value-add).

## What counsel *does* own — the legal opinion

Counsel's actual expertise, and the value-add they bring
that a founder cannot replace:

- **Securities-law compliance.** Whether the round documents
  are compliant with US federal securities law (Regulation D
  most commonly) and the applicable state blue-sky laws.
  <!-- needs-research: primary-source citation for Regulation D safe harbors under Rule 506(b) and Rule 506(c); SEC.gov is the authoritative source. -->
- **Corporate document consistency.** Whether the certificate
  of incorporation amendment, the stock purchase agreement,
  the investor rights agreement, and any side letters are
  internally consistent and consistent with the term sheet.
- **409A valuation implications.** Whether the priced round
  triggers a new 409A requirement for common-stock strike
  prices going forward.
- **Tax-election reminders.** Whether founders' 83(b)
  elections are on file and current; whether any grants
  since the last round need 83(b) filings.
- **Delaware-specific corporate-law issues.** For a Delaware
  C-corp (the modal seed-stage structure), any state-
  specific corporate-law issues the round documents
  interact with.
- **Fiduciary-duty flags.** Whether any conflicts of
  interest, related-party transactions, or director-
  approval requirements exist that the founder should know
  about.

None of this is founder-authored. All of it is what the
founder is paying counsel to do.

## Choosing counsel

Two constraints on choosing seed-stage counsel:

- **Firm has to have real venture practice.** Not "a firm
  that does venture deals sometimes." A firm whose partners
  routinely close seed and Series A rounds and knows the
  NVCA model documents and the current YC SAFE forms cold.
- **Firm has to have startup-friendly billing.** Some firms
  will defer fees to the priced round's close, some will
  offer capped-fee packages for a seed round. Ask upfront;
  don't assume.

Cooley, Wilson Sonsini, Fenwick, Gunderson, Orrick, and DLA
Piper are historic Silicon Valley venture firms; regional and
newer firms are also common. This module doesn't recommend
specific counsel — the point is that "startup lawyer at a
generalist firm" is a category error at seed.

## The round-close mechanics — where counsel and founder split

Once the term sheet is signed, the round-close mechanics
divide roughly like this:

- **Founder owns.** Cap-table maintenance in the cap-table
  service (Carta, Pulley, etc.); investor updates during the
  close window (from mod-004 chapter 08); the wire tracking;
  the "email the passers" close-comms.
- **Counsel owns.** Drafting the round documents (cert of
  inc amendment, SPA, IRA, ROFR/co-sale, voting agreement,
  side letters); running the closing checklist; confirming
  securities-law compliance; landing the signatures.
- **Both own together.** The final cap-table walk
  post-close (founder produces from the cap-table service;
  counsel confirms consistency with the executed documents).

The founder should not draft the round documents. Counsel
should not maintain the cap-table service. Confusion between
these two roles is a common source of avoidable friction
during the close.

## Common wrong versions

- **"My lawyer will handle it."** Founder hands over the
  term sheet without a redline or a cap-table walk. Counsel
  bills 3× as much and delivers a generic opinion. Founder
  learns nothing and signs a round they can't defend.
- **"I don't need counsel for a SAFE."** Common at pre-seed.
  A single SAFE from a repeat investor at the YC standard
  form is genuinely low-risk; a stack of custom SAFEs with
  side letters from angels who each have their own counsel
  is not. When in doubt, spend the $500–1,500 for counsel
  to review.
- **Cap-table walk that doesn't sum to 100%.** Handed to
  counsel, this becomes a $2k billable exercise as counsel
  reproduces the walk from scratch. Get to 100.0% before
  the handoff.
- **Redlines written as "I don't like this clause."** Vague
  redlines produce vague counter-proposals from the lead.
  Every redline should specify the new language you want,
  even if counsel will refine the wording.
- **Counsel asked to render a business judgement.** "Should
  I take this term sheet?" is not a legal question. Counsel
  can tell you the terms are above / below market; the
  decision to sign is yours.

## Summary

- Seed-round paperwork produces two founder-authored
  artifacts (**cap-table walk**, **term-sheet redline**) and
  one counsel-authored deliverable (**legal opinion**).
- **The founder authors the arithmetic and the redline.**
  Outsourcing them to counsel loses defensibility, costs
  more, and forfeits the founder's information position for
  every subsequent round.
- **Counsel delivers the legal opinion** — securities-law
  compliance, corporate-document consistency, 409A
  implications, tax-election reminders, fiduciary-duty
  flags, state-specific corporate-law issues. This is the
  actual counsel value-add.
- Choose counsel with a real venture practice and startup-
  friendly billing. "Generalist firm that does startup
  deals sometimes" is a category error at seed.
- Hand counsel the cap-table walk, the redline, and a
  specific ask — not the raw term sheet. The bill will be
  smaller and the opinion will be sharper.
