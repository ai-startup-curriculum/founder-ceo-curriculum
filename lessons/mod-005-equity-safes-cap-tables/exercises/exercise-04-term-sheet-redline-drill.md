# Exercise 04 — Term-Sheet Redline Drill

**Module:** 005 Equity, SAFEs & Cap Tables · **Stage:** SEED ·
**Time:** ~2 focused hours · **Reads with:** chapters
[08](../08-term-sheet-clauses-liq-pref-anti-dilution.md) and
[09](../09-briefing-counsel-and-the-redline.md).

## Problem statement

Reading a term sheet for the first time is a lot like reading
a legal contract for the first time in any domain: most of the
words go past, and the specific clauses that reach into your
company's math are buried in dense paragraphs. The two clauses
that reach into the cap-table math — **liquidation preference**
and **anti-dilution** — have well-established defaults (chapter
08). Deviations from those defaults are worth spotting on sight
and pushing back on with specific redlines.

This exercise gives you a term-sheet excerpt with several
deliberate deviations from market defaults. Your job is to
identify each one, produce a specific redline, quantify the
founder-side cost (in dollars at a stated exit or in pp at a
stated down-round scenario), and produce a counsel briefing
that hands the redline to your lawyer for drafting.

## Deliverables (two founder artifacts, plus one call)

1. **A marked-up term sheet** — the excerpt below with each
   deviation from market annotated, the specific redline
   written out, and a one-sentence rationale for each.
2. **A one-page "cost of the deviations" note** — dollar
   impact at a stated $50M exit (for liquidation preference
   redlines) and pp impact at a stated down-round scenario
   (for anti-dilution redlines).
3. **A counsel briefing** — the one-paragraph ask you send
   your lawyer with the marked-up term sheet attached,
   specifying what you want counsel to do and what stays
   with you.

## The term-sheet excerpt

> *This is a stylised excerpt for drill purposes only. It is
> not a real term sheet; do not use it as a template for
> negotiating an actual round.*

**Series Seed Preferred Stock Financing — Summary of
Principal Terms**

**Issuer:** [Company Inc.]

**Investors:** Lead Investor and other Series Seed investors
(the "Investors").

**Amount:** $2,000,000

**Valuation:** $8,000,000 pre-money, $10,000,000 post-money
(inclusive of the Option Pool described below).

**Option Pool:** An Option Pool equal to **18%** of the
post-Closing fully-diluted capitalization shall be reserved
prior to the Closing. Refreshes at future rounds to be
determined by the Board of Directors.

**Liquidation Preference:** Each share of Series Seed
Preferred shall have a liquidation preference equal to
**2×** the Original Issue Price, plus any declared but
unpaid dividends. The Series Seed Preferred shall
**participate** with the Common on an as-converted basis
in any remaining proceeds after payment of the liquidation
preference, without cap.

**Anti-Dilution:** The conversion price of the Series Seed
Preferred shall be adjusted on a **full-ratchet** basis in
the event of any subsequent issuance of shares at a lower
effective price per share. Standard exclusions apply for
option-pool grants and shares issued in connection with
acquisitions.

**Board Composition:** The Board shall consist of three
members: one Series Seed director designated by the Lead
Investor, one Common director designated by the Common
holders, and one Independent director to be mutually
agreed by the Lead Investor and the Common holders.

**Protective Provisions:** Standard NVCA-form protective
provisions apply.

**Pro Rata Rights:** Each Series Seed investor shall have
the right to participate on a pro rata basis in any future
issuance of equity securities.

**Information Rights:** Standard quarterly financial
reporting and annual audited financials (if available).

## Requirements

- **Identify every deviation** from the market defaults in
  chapter 08. There are (at least) **three** in the excerpt
  above. Mark each on the excerpt.
- **Write a specific redline** for each deviation — the exact
  language you want the clause to say. "Change to market
  standard" is not a redline; the specific replacement
  language is.
- **Quantify the cost** at a stated scenario:
  - For liquidation-preference deviations: dollar impact at a
    **$50M exit** (using the standard-scenario cap table from
    chapter 06 or your own).
  - For anti-dilution deviations: pp impact on the Series
    Seed's conversion price at a **stated down round** — e.g.,
    a $4M raise at a 30% price drop from the original
    conversion price.
- **Prioritise your redlines.** Not every deviation is
  equally worth negotiation credit. Rank them 1 (highest
  priority — refuse to sign without) to N (lowest priority —
  would accept if trading it for something more important).
- **Write the counsel briefing.** One paragraph handed to
  your lawyer with the marked-up term sheet attached. Specify
  what you want counsel to do (draft the specific language,
  confirm the arithmetic, identify additional legal or tax
  exposure) and what stays with you (the business decision
  on which redlines to hold firm on).

## Starter guidance

**Session 1 (~30 min) — read against the defaults.** Read
each numbered clause. For each one, ask: is this the default
from chapter 08? If not, what does it deviate to? Mark the
deviations without yet writing redlines.

**Session 2 (~45 min) — write the redlines and cost them.**
For each deviation, write the specific replacement language.
Then compute the cost:

- **Liquidation preference at $50M exit.** Assume the
  standard-scenario cap table (chapter 06): Series Seed owns
  20% of the post-close FD from a $2M / $10M-post round;
  founders combined own 62%. Compute what Series Seed
  receives and what common receives under the term-sheet
  clause vs under the 1×-non-participating default at exits
  of $50M, $10M, and $8M. Report the delta at each.

- **Anti-dilution at a down round.** Assume the down round
  is $4M raised at a price 30% below the original Series
  Seed conversion price. Compute the Series Seed's new
  conversion price under (a) full-ratchet as in the term
  sheet, and (b) broad-based weighted-average as in the
  default. Report the delta in Series Seed effective
  ownership at post-close-of-down-round.

**Session 3 (~30 min) — prioritise and brief counsel.** Rank
your redlines. Which one, if you had to accept just one
deviation from your ideal, would you concede? Write the
counsel briefing.

## Acceptance criteria

You are done when *all* of the following are true:

- [ ] Every clause deviating from market default has been
      identified and marked.
- [ ] Each deviation has a **specific redline** (not "change
      to market") with replacement language.
- [ ] Each redline has a one-sentence rationale.
- [ ] Cost of the liquidation-preference deviations is
      computed at $50M, $10M, and $8M exits.
- [ ] Cost of the anti-dilution deviation is computed as a
      pp delta at the stated down-round scenario.
- [ ] Redlines are prioritised (1 to N) so counsel and the
      lead know which are non-negotiable.
- [ ] Counsel briefing states what counsel does (draft, confirm,
      identify exposure) and what stays with the founder
      (the business decision).

## Self-assessment rubric

| Dimension | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Deviation identification | Missed one or more deviations | All deviations from chapter-08 defaults spotted, including subtler ones |
| Redline specificity | "Change to market" or vague | Specific replacement language for each clause |
| Rationale | "Because it's not market" | One-sentence rationale tied to founder cost or downside risk |
| Cost computation (liq pref) | Impact hand-waved | Dollar deltas computed at at least three exit prices |
| Cost computation (anti-dilution) | Full-ratchet vs weighted-average compared qualitatively only | pp delta computed at a specific down-round scenario using the weighted-average formula |
| Prioritisation | Redlines listed without priority | Ranked 1 to N with reasoning; the "would walk from the round" line clearly stated |
| Counsel handoff | Term sheet handed to counsel with "is this fine?" | Specific ask; founder-authored redline; separation of business decision from legal drafting |

## Definition of done

A skeptical seed-stage venture lawyer, reading your marked-up
term sheet and counsel briefing, would agree that (a) every
deviation from market defaults has been spotted and redlined,
(b) the specific replacement language you want is unambiguous
enough for them to draft, (c) the founder-side cost is
quantified at both an exit and a down-round scenario, and (d)
you have prioritised your redlines clearly enough that the
subsequent negotiation call is a series of specific asks, not
a re-read of the term sheet.
