# Exercise 03 — Option Pool Sizing Tied to the Hiring Plan

**Module:** 005 Equity, SAFEs & Cap Tables · **Stage:**
PRE-SEED→SEED · **Time:** ~2–3 focused hours · **Reads with:**
chapter [05](../05-option-pool-sizing-and-who-pays.md); depends
on [mod-003 chapter 04 (18-month operating model)](../../mod-003-runway-and-financial-modeling/04-eighteen-month-operating-model.md)
and [mod-003 exercise 01](../../mod-003-runway-and-financial-modeling/exercises/exercise-01-eighteen-month-operating-plan.md).

## Problem statement

Every institutional seed round expects a pool. The lead's counsel
usually types **15%** as the default. Your founders' equity says
15% is worth **two to three percentage points per founder** at
close (chapter 07). The question is whether you can defend a
smaller number — 10%, say — with something a partner cannot
argue with.

The answer is a **hiring plan with grants attached**. This
exercise produces exactly that: a named list of hires you
expect to make between round close and the next round, with a
standard-band grant against each, a stated buffer, and a
resulting pool percentage the founder can bring to the term
sheet.

## Deliverables (three founder artifacts)

1. **A pool-sizing sheet** listing every planned hire from
   round close to the next round, with role, expected start
   month, and expected option grant. Sums to a defensible pool
   percentage of post-close FD.
2. **A one-paragraph "why this number" note** — the reasoning
   for the pool percentage, tied to specific hires and to a
   named buffer for unnamed hires.
3. **A one-paragraph "what if the lead pushes for X%" script**
   — the specific counter you make when the lead's counsel
   opens with a different number, and the reasoning that
   defends yours.

## Scenario

Pick one:

- **Your real startup** (recommended if you're an operating
  founder): use your **actual** 18-month operating plan from
  mod-003 exercise 01. Every hire in that plan has a start
  month and a role; assign each an expected grant using the
  standard grant bands in chapter 05.
- **Simulated:** Your mod-003 18-month plan calls for the
  following hires between round close and the next round:
  - Engineer #1 (senior full-stack) — starts month 3.
  - Engineer #2 (mid, backend) — starts month 6.
  - Head of Engineering / CTO-caliber non-founder hire — starts
    month 9.
  - Design lead — starts month 12.
  - Two IC hires (mid-level) — start months 14 and 16.
  - Two senior advisors — attached at close.

  The lead is asking for a **15% post-close pool** based on
  their template. Your job is to produce a sizing that maps
  every share to a specific person or a stated buffer.

## Requirements

- **Hire-by-hire grants.** Every planned hire has a specific
  expected grant percentage of post-close FD. Use the standard
  grant bands from chapter 05 as your reference:

  | Role                                          | Typical grant at seed |
  |-----------------------------------------------|-----------------------|
  | Early engineer #1–3                           | 0.5–2.0%              |
  | Head of Engineering / CTO hire (non-founder)  | 1.0–5.0%              |
  | VP-level first hire                           | 1.0–3.0%              |
  | Design / marketing lead                       | 0.5–1.5%              |
  | Individual contributor #4–15                  | 0.1–0.5%              |
  | Senior advisor (per person)                   | 0.1–0.5%              |

  <!-- needs-research: primary-source citation for these grant bands; Carta employee-equity data, Kruze Consulting's option-grant benchmarks, and Cooley GO's compensation surveys are common anchors. Ranges shift by role, stage, and sector. -->

- **Named buffer.** State a specific buffer (typically 1–2%)
  for hires you can't yet name. Explain briefly what the
  buffer covers (surprise hire, refresh grant, etc.).
- **Sum to pool %.** Sum all planned grants + buffer to a
  target pool percentage. This is the number you defend.
- **Compare to lead's ask.** If the lead is asking for a
  different percentage, quantify the pp difference and
  translate that into per-founder dilution using the standard
  scenario math from chapter 06.
- **Live-lab check** (if using your real startup): compare
  your sizing to what the lead has actually put in front of
  you. If they diverge, name the specific hires (or the
  specific buffer) the difference implies.

## Starter guidance

**Session 1 (~30 min) — inputs.** Open your mod-003 operating
plan (or the simulated hiring list). List every hire from
round close to the next round, with role and start month.

**Session 2 (~60 min) — grants and buffer.** For each hire,
assign a grant percentage from the standard bands. Err
slightly high on senior hires (CTO, VP, design lead) — those
are the negotiable ones where a stronger grant may be needed
to close a candidate. Err low on IC hires — those are the
grants a compensation refresh will address later.

Add a **buffer** of 1–2%: a modest catch-all for hires the
plan didn't name and for refresh grants to early hires. State
what the buffer covers.

Sum the grants + buffer. That is your **defensible pool
percentage of post-close FD**.

**Session 3 (~45 min) — dilution math and script.** If the
lead's ask is X% and yours is Y%, the pp difference (X − Y)
is roughly the pp swing on founder ownership at close. In
the standard scenario (chapter 06), a 5 pp increase in pool
translates to about **2 pp per founder** at close.

Write the "why this number" note and the "counter" script.
The script should:
- Acknowledge the lead's ask.
- Present the hiring plan with grants attached.
- Show that the plan supports your number, not theirs.
- Offer a specific commitment on refresh at Series A if that
  removes the lead's safety-margin objection.

## Acceptance criteria

You are done when *all* of the following are true:

- [ ] Every planned hire has a name / role, start month, and
      expected grant %.
- [ ] Buffer is stated with a percentage and a one-sentence
      description of what it covers.
- [ ] Sum of grants + buffer = the defended pool %.
- [ ] "Why this number" paragraph ties the number to specific
      hires and reasoning.
- [ ] "Counter script" quantifies the pp swing between your
      number and the lead's, and offers a specific alternative
      (refresh at Series A) if pushback lands.
- [ ] Grant percentages are within (or defensibly outside) the
      standard bands from chapter 05.

## Self-assessment rubric

| Dimension | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Hire-plan grounding | Pool sized to a vibe | Every share mapped to a specific hire or a stated buffer |
| Standard-band awareness | Grants pulled from thin air | Grants tied to seed-stage bands with a reason if outside |
| Buffer discipline | Silent implicit buffer | Buffer stated with a percentage and a one-sentence rationale |
| Dilution translation | Pool % discussed in isolation | pp change on founders quantified using chapter 06 math |
| Counter script | "We'd like a smaller pool" | Specific, defensible counter with an alternative (refresh at Series A) |
| Live-lab realism | Simulated hiring only | Real 18-month plan from mod-003 with actual planned hires |

## Definition of done

A skeptical seed-stage lead partner, reading your pool-sizing
sheet and script, would agree that (a) every share in the
pool corresponds to a specific hire or a stated buffer, (b)
the resulting pool percentage is smaller than the counsel-
default 15% *because the plan supports a smaller number*, and
(c) you have a defensible fallback (refresh at Series A) if
they push for more.
