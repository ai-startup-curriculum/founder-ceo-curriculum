# Chapter 05 — Option Pool Sizing: Tie It to the Hiring Plan; Know Who Pays

> **Reads with:** module objective 4 — *size an option pool (~10–15%
> at seed, refreshed pre-money) tied to the mod-003 hiring plan
> rather than the round-number the lead's counsel typed first, and
> understand that the pool comes out of existing shareholders'
> stake, not the new investor's.*

## The pool is the biggest structural lever the founder still has

The valuation is often the number founders talk about at seed. The
**option pool** is the number that moves founder ownership more.
A three-percentage-point difference in pool size — 10% vs 13%,
say — is a three-percentage-point difference in founder dilution
at close, on top of whatever the round takes.

That is because of two facts most first-time founders miss:

- The pool is refreshed **pre-money** at the round, by market
  convention — meaning the pool sits *inside* the pre-money
  valuation.
- Every share reserved in that pre-money refresh comes out of the
  **existing shareholders'** stake, not the new investor's.

Put together: **the founder pays for the option pool the new
investor demands.** The size of that pool is negotiable; the
convention that founders pay is not. Understanding both is what
lets you defend a smaller, hiring-plan-sized pool at the term
sheet.

## What the pool is for

An **option pool** is a block of shares reserved for future grants
to employees, contractors, and advisors. It is not a business
line item; it is not a marketing device; it is a **hiring
instrument**. The purpose of the pool is to have enough shares
available to grant, at competitive levels, to every person you
will hire between now and the next round — no more and no less.

Grants come out of the pool as hires are made. When the pool
runs low, it is **refreshed** at the next round, usually back to
some target percentage of post-close FD.

If you can't say what your pool will be used to grant, and to
whom, over the next 18–24 months, then whatever percentage number
sits in the pool is arbitrary — and, at the seed round, that
arbitrariness costs the founders directly.

## Sizing to the mod-003 hiring plan (not the counsel-typed round number)

The right size for the pool is the size the company will
*actually grant* between now and the next round, plus a modest
buffer for hires you can't yet name. The reference model is the
one you already built in mod-003:

- **Named hires with start months.** From the 18-month operating
  model in mod-003 chapter 04, the plan already lists who you
  intend to hire and when. Every named hire has an expected
  option grant that scales with role seniority.
- **Standard grant bands.** At seed, standard grant bands land
  roughly in these ranges (US-market, illustrative; sector and
  geography move them):

  | Role                          | Typical grant at seed |
  |-------------------------------|-----------------------|
  | Early engineer #1–3           | 0.5–2.0%              |
  | Head of Engineering / CTO hire (non-founder) | 1.0–5.0%   |
  | VP-level first hire           | 1.0–3.0%              |
  | Design / marketing lead       | 0.5–1.5%              |
  | Individual contributor #4–15  | 0.1–0.5%              |
  | Senior advisor (per person)   | 0.1–0.5%              |

- **Buffer for unnamed hires and refresh grants.** A small
  buffer (1–2% of the pool total) covers a hire you didn't
  plan for or a refresh grant to an early hire.

Multiply each planned hire by their expected grant, sum, add the
buffer: that is the **defensible pool size**. Present it to the
lead as a **hiring plan with grants attached**, not as a
percentage. The plan is a spreadsheet; the percentage is what
falls out of it.

A worked example, using the mod-003 illustrative plan:

- Two engineers already planned (mod-003 exercise 01 scenario)
  at 1.5% each → **3.0%**.
- One head-of-engineering hire the lead expects the round to
  fund at 3.0% → **3.0%**.
- Two IC hires at 0.5% each → **1.0%**.
- Two advisor grants at 0.25% each → **0.5%**.
- Buffer of 1.5% → **1.5%**.

Total: **9.0% of post-close FD**. Round to 10% for negotiating
symmetry and you have a defensible pool that maps to eight named
hires and a stated buffer.

## The pre-money refresh convention

At a priced round, the lead investor almost always demands that
the pool be **topped up ("refreshed") pre-money**. Two things
about this convention matter:

- **"Pre-money" means the pool sits inside the pre-money
  valuation.** If the pre-money is $8M and the round is $2M, the
  post-money is $10M, and the pool sits inside the $8M.
- **The refresh target is a percentage of post-close FD.**
  "10% post-close pool" means the pool is 10% of the FD table
  *after* the round closes.

The math consequence: because the pool is inside the $8M
pre-money and is sized as a percentage of the $10M post-close,
the pool has to be a slightly larger percentage of the pre-money
than of the post-close. The pool comes out of the pre-money
holders — founders plus any converting SAFEs — and dilutes them
before the new-money investor's dollar is counted.

Concretely, in the standard scenario (two founders, $500k
post-money SAFE at $5M cap, $2M at $8M pre-money, 10% post-close
pool):

- The new-money investor buys **20%** of the post-close FD
  (`$2M / $10M`).
- The pool is **10%** of the post-close FD, sitting inside the
  pre-money.
- The SAFE holder converts to **10%** of the pre-new-money FD
  (invariant), which is `10% × (1 − 20%) = 8%` of post-close.
- The founders are left with `100% − 10% − 10% × (1 − 20%) −
  20% = 100% − 10% − 8% − 20% = 62%` of post-close, split
  50/50 → **31% each**.

The pool cost the founders roughly **10 percentage points** of
post-close ownership. If the same round had been done with **no
pool refresh**, the founders would have ended up at roughly
**36% each** — a 5-pp swing per founder. Chapter 06 walks the
share-by-share version.

## Who pays: existing holders, not the incoming investor

The founder-critical fact about the pre-money refresh convention:
**the pool dilutes the existing shareholders, not the incoming
investor**. The lead investor buys their `r%` of the post-close
FD; the pool sits inside the pre-money, so the pool comes out of
whoever held pre-money shares — founders and any converted SAFE
holders.

That is why the lead cares about pool size. Every extra
percentage point of pool is a percentage point the founders (and
existing SAFE holders) fund, not the lead. The lead ends up with
the same percentage of post-close FD whether the pool is 10% or
15%; only the founders' number moves.

The corollary is the founder's lever: **push back on pool size,
not on pool convention.** The convention is standard at
essentially every institutional fund. The size is where the
money is.

## Defending a smaller pool at the term sheet

Two things move a lead off "the standard 15% post-close pool" to
a smaller number:

- **A named hiring plan.** If your pool sizing document lists
  every hire, their expected grant, and a stated buffer — and
  the total is 10% — the lead has to argue with the plan, not
  the founder. Plans are harder to argue with than percentages.
- **A commitment to refresh at Series A if needed.** Leads
  demand large pools partly because they don't want to
  re-authorize a pool between rounds. A commitment to refresh
  at Series A (with the founders again bearing the pre-money
  cost, per convention) removes the "safety margin" argument
  for oversizing today.

Two things that don't work:

- **Arguing that the pool convention is unfair.** It is, and it
  is universal. Fighting the convention marks you as
  un-oriented to the market.
- **Refusing to size the pool.** Every institutional round will
  refuse to close without a pool. Not having a sizing document
  is a good way to have the number chosen for you.

## Grants come out of the pool as hires happen

After the round closes, grants are made from the pool as hires
happen. On the cap table:

- **Granted options** move from the reserved-pool row to the
  granted row. The FD share count doesn't change; the pool
  shrinks and granted rises by the same amount.
- **Options exercised** convert into common. Granted-option row
  shrinks; common row grows. FD share count still doesn't
  change (the option was already in the FD share count as a
  reserved-then-granted right).
- **Options that never vest** (a grantee leaves before their
  cliff) return to the pool. The reserved row grows by the
  unvested amount.

The pool is a working balance. A **healthy startup at
month 18** has granted most of the pool to actual hires, with a
small residual reserved for the next few hires — and is negotiating
the pool refresh at the Series A term sheet against a fresh 18-month
hiring plan.

## Common failure modes

- **The 15% pool the lead's counsel typed first.** The lead's
  legal template defaults to 15%. If the founder doesn't come
  in with a hiring-plan-based number, 15% is what gets signed
  — and that is 3–5 percentage points of avoidable founder
  dilution.
- **A pool sized to a vibe.** "We need 12% because that feels
  right." That is not defensible against a lead who wants 15%;
  neither is it defensible against your own future. Tie the
  number to hires.
- **A pool not refreshed at Series A.** Some founders resist
  the Series A pool refresh, hoping to avoid the dilution. This
  usually means running out of grantable shares mid-year and
  having to authorize a pool between rounds — which requires
  board consent, dilutes everyone, and is a bad conversation
  to have with new employees waiting for their grants.
- **The pool used to fund non-employee equity.** Grants to
  investors, service providers billed in equity, or friend-of-
  the-CEO grants come out of the same pool. Every one of those
  is founder dilution funding a decision that has nothing to do
  with hiring. Route non-hiring equity through a different
  authorization if possible.

## Summary

- The option pool is a **hiring instrument**. Size it to the
  mod-003 hiring plan — named hires, standard grant bands,
  small buffer — not to the round-number the lead's counsel
  typed first.
- Typical seed pool is **10–15% of post-close FD**, refreshed at
  each priced round. The right specific number falls out of the
  hiring plan; if 10% supports the plan, defend 10%.
