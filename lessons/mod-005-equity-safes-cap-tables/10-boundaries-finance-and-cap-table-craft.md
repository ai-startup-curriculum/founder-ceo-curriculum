## Why the boundaries exist

Founder/CEO curriculum modules are role pathways over shared
functional curricula, not a separate body of knowledge (see the
top-level `README.md` and `PREREQUISITES.md`). The founder learns
*enough of every function to run the company through the seed
round*; the deeper craft of each function is owned by the
pathway for the role that will eventually do it full-time. Cap-
table craft and equity compensation follow the same rule.

- **This module (`founder-ceo/mod-005-equity-safes-cap-tables`)**
  teaches you to read and author a **seed-stage cap table**: the
  four share classes, the SAFE (anatomy and post-money
  conversion arithmetic), the option pool tied to a hiring
  plan, the walked example from founding through a priced seed,
  the three structural decisions, the two term-sheet clauses
  that reach into the cap-table math, and the counsel handoff.
- **The startup-finance-fundraising track
  (`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`, level 30)** teaches
  the deeper craft of **post-Series-A cap-table administration,
  employee-stock-plan operations, 409A valuations, secondary
  transactions, and CFO-scale round mechanics** — the work a
  CFO, a stock-plan administrator, and specialist counsel do
  once the company is large enough that these functions are
  full-time jobs.

If you are the founder-CEO of a pre-Series-A company, most of
what this module covers is your job today (with counsel and a
cap-table service filling in the specialist paper you can't
reach yourself). Once you land the seed and hire a first
finance leader, and once you have real stock-plan grants going
out to a growing headcount, the deeper craft transfers — but
the material in this module remains the founder's baseline
understanding of *how the cap table works* and *what the
levers are*.

## What "the seed-stage cap table" covers, and what it deliberately doesn't

This module is scoped to the founder artifact that carries every
seed-stage equity decision: **the fully-diluted cap table walked
from founding through a priced seed, and the term-sheet
redline that accompanies it.**

Explicitly **in** scope for this module:

- **What a cap table is** — issued vs FD, ownership arithmetic,
  the discipline of summing to 100.0%
  ([chapter 01](./01-cap-table-fundamentals.md)).
- **The four share classes** — common, preferred (with its
  default rights), options (granted and reserved), convertibles
  on the stack ([chapter 02](./02-four-share-classes.md)).
- **The SAFE anatomy** — cap, discount, MFN, pre-money vs
  post-money form ([chapter 03](./03-safe-anatomy.md)).
- **Post-money SAFE conversion math** — the amount/cap
  invariant, stack sums, the stackable form for what happens
  after new money is added
  ([chapter 04](./04-post-money-safe-conversion-math.md)).
- **Option pool sizing** tied to the mod-003 hiring plan, and
  the pre-money refresh convention that makes founders pay for
  the pool
  ([chapter 05](./05-option-pool-sizing-and-who-pays.md)).
- **The walked example** from founding through a priced seed
  ([chapter 06](./06-cap-table-walk-founding-to-priced-seed.md)).
- **The three structural decisions** that most move founder
  ownership — pool size, SAFE cap and stack, round size and
  pre-money together
  ([chapter 07](./07-three-structural-decisions.md)).
- **Two term-sheet clauses** that reach into the cap-table math
  — liquidation preference and anti-dilution
  ([chapter 08](./08-term-sheet-clauses-liq-pref-anti-dilution.md)).
- **Briefing counsel** with the founder-authored cap-table
  walk and term-sheet redline; where the founder / counsel
  handoff is
  ([chapter 09](./09-briefing-counsel-and-the-redline.md)).

## The boundary to `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` (level 30)

Explicitly **out** of scope for this module — deferred to the
startup-finance-fundraising track:

- **Deep priced-round mechanics beyond the seed.** Series B, C,
  and later; multiple senior series with their own preference
  stacks; complex participation caps; preferred-vote-with-
  common vs preferred-vote-separately mechanics on major
  decisions; multi-round anti-dilution interactions. This
  module treats the seed and Series A defaults;
  post-Series-A rounds involve stacks of clauses this module
  doesn't cover.
- **Employee stock plan administration.** Grant plan design,
  new-hire grant policy, refresh-grant policy, promotion
  grants, tender-offer administration, plan-level tax
  compliance, and the specific mechanics of exercising and
  settling options at scale. This module treats the pool as
  a percentage-of-post-close number; administering the plan
  is a full-time role beyond seed.

the finance track treats in a chapter or two.** If you are
hiring the CFO who will run the Series B, or if you are the
CFO reading this module to align with the founder, the depth
you need is in `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`, not
here.

## The boundary to `startup-operations-governance-curriculum` (level 50)

Cap-table decisions do not sit in isolation from board and
governance decisions. Two adjacent boundaries worth naming:

- **Board approval of the cap table.** Every major cap-table
  event — a pool refresh, a priced round, a secondary
  transaction — requires board approval and the associated
  paperwork (unanimous written consents, meeting minutes,
  resolutions). This module covers *what* the cap-table
  events look like; the governance track covers *how* they
  are formally approved by a functioning board.
- **Executive compensation as a board-approved artifact.**
  CEO compensation, executive option refresh grants, and
  compensation-committee-scoped decisions all touch the
  cap table but are governance decisions above them. The
  governance track owns compensation-committee mechanics.

## What the founder-CEO still owes the cap-table function, always

The boundary transfers the *craft* to the peer tracks. It does
not transfer the *judgement* — that is permanently CEO work.
The founder-CEO's ongoing obligations, at every stage of the
company, are:

- **Own the cap-table walk.** No matter how large the finance
  function grows, the CEO who cannot state the current FD
  cap table from memory — founders, pool, converted SAFEs,
  each series' ownership — has outsourced something no CFO
  or counsel should own alone.
- **Own the structural asks in each round.** The pool size,
  the SAFE cap, the round size and pre-money together, the
  liquidation preference and anti-dilution redlines. Every
  round's structural asks are business trade-offs, not legal
  mechanics; the CEO owns them.
- **Own the counsel relationship at cap-table decisions.**
  Counsel is a service provider; the CEO decides what to
  ask counsel to do and how to weigh the legal opinion
  against the business context. Post-Series-A this may
  transfer partially to a CFO, but the CEO stays in the
  room.
- **Refuse to let the cap table become opaque.** A cap-
  table service producing beautiful reports that the CEO
  doesn't personally understand is a dangerous condition.
  The CEO periodically re-deriving the current FD % from
  raw share counts is a permanent responsibility.

## When to graduate from this module to the peer tracks

Three signals it's time to move the deeper cap-table work
from "founder does it themselves at 11pm" to the finance
track:

- **First real finance hire in seat.** A Head of Finance /
  CFO changes what the founder should own personally. Hand
  off the cap-table maintenance, the round documents, and
  the 409A cadence as the starting corpus; the CFO will
  build in their own tooling. From that point, the finance
  track's material is the standard the finance function is
  being held to.
- **Series B or later round.** The paperwork, the diligence
  workstream, and the specialist-counsel involvement scale
  meaningfully at Series B. A Series A run out of this
  module alone (with good counsel) is legal; a Series B
  run out of this module alone is under-prepared.
- **First tender offer or secondary transaction.** Secondary
  liquidity for employees or early investors triggers a
  category of paperwork this module doesn't teach.

Before those signals, this module's material is sufficient.
After them, this module's material remains true — the four
share classes, the SAFE invariant, the pool-refresh math, the
term-sheet defaults, the counsel handoff don't stop being
right — but the operating pattern they live inside is
different, and that is what the peer tracks teach.

## Summary

- This module ends at the **seed-stage cap table and the
  first priced round** — the four share classes, the SAFE
  arithmetic, the pool sizing, the walked example, the three
  structural decisions, the two term-sheet clauses that
  reach into the cap table, and the counsel handoff.
- **Deep priced-round mechanics beyond the seed, employee-
  stock-plan administration, 409A valuations, secondary
  transactions, QSBS and founder tax planning, and complex
  preferred conversion in M&A** belong to the
  **`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` (level 30)**
  peer track. Defer to it once a finance function exists or
  the round is Series B or later.
- **Board approval of cap-table events and executive
  compensation** belong to the
  **`startup-operations-governance-curriculum` (level 50)**
  peer track.
- Transfer *craft* to the peer tracks; keep *judgement*
  (the structural asks, the counsel relationship, the
  cap-table walk in the CEO's head) permanently with the
  CEO.
