# Chapter 08 — Boundaries: The Startup-Product-GTM Track and Legal Counsel

> **Reads with:** module objectives 8 and 9 —
> *cite the boundary to `startup-product-gtm-curriculum` (level
> 30) for post-founder-led GTM — first SDR/AE hire, quota-
> carrying reps, marketing engine, formal sales org, channel
> strategy; and cite the boundary to legal counsel for MSA / DPA
> / BAA / procurement-paper authoring — the CEO negotiates and
> signs, counsel drafts and reviews.*

## Why the boundaries exist

Founder/CEO curriculum modules are role pathways over shared
functional curricula, not separate bodies of knowledge (see
the top-level `README.md` and `PREREQUISITES.md`). The
founder learns *enough of every function to run the company
through the seed round*; the deeper craft of each function is
owned by the pathway for the role that will eventually do it
full-time. Sales and GTM follow the same rule. So does the
legal paper that sales produces.

This chapter names two boundaries:

- To the **`startup-product-gtm-curriculum`** peer track
  (level 30), for everything that comes *after* the
  transition signal in chapter 07 — the first sales hire,
  quota-carrying reps, marketing engine, formal sales org,
  channel strategy.
- To **legal counsel**, for the drafting and legal review
  of MSA, DPA, BAA, and procurement paper — the paper the
  CEO negotiates and signs but does not author from
  scratch.

Both boundaries are permanent. Both leave the *judgement*
with the CEO, transferring only the *craft* to the specialist.

## Boundary 1 — `startup-product-gtm-curriculum` (level 30)

Explicitly **out** of scope for this module — deferred to
the startup-product-gtm track:

- **The first SDR/AE hire mechanics.** Sourcing profiles,
  interview loops, comp plan design, quota setting,
  onboarding and ramp plans, the 90-day plan a new AE
  should execute. This module names *when* to hire (the
  transition signal in chapter 07); the peer track owns
  *how* to hire, comp, and ramp.
- **Quota-carrying rep management.** Territory design,
  account assignment, quota progression, coaching cadence,
  pipeline reviews at scale, deal-desk mechanics. This
  module runs the deals with a single founder-closer; the
  peer track runs deals across a sales team.
- **Marketing engine.** Content marketing, SEO, paid
  acquisition, ABM (account-based marketing), demand
  generation, MQL/SQL frameworks, marketing operations,
  brand development. This module has no marketing engine
  because it doesn't need one at the scale it operates;
  the peer track builds and runs the engine.
- **Formal sales organization.** Sales ops, sales
  enablement, sales engineering, customer success as a
  formal function, RevOps stack (Salesforce / HubSpot /
  Outreach / Gong administration). This module operates
  with a founder + spreadsheet + a light CRM; the peer
  track builds and runs the org.
- **Channel strategy.** Reseller partnerships, systems
  integrators, marketplace listings (AWS Marketplace,
  Salesforce AppExchange, Azure Marketplace, GCP
  Marketplace), OEM deals, referral programs at scale.
  This module sells direct; the peer track adds channels.
- **Pricing and packaging as a permanent GTM discipline.**
  Multi-tier pricing, usage-based pricing model design,
  enterprise-tier packaging, pricing governance across a
  sales team. Mod-002 owns the first pricing hypothesis
  and this module updates it with real sales data; the
  peer track owns pricing as an ongoing GTM function.
- **Customer success and retention as an engine.** Onboarding
  playbooks at scale, NPS/CSAT programs, churn analysis,
  expansion motion, customer advisory boards, professional
  services. This module gets the first customers
  successful; the peer track builds a customer success
  organization.
- **Sales analytics and forecasting.** Pipeline stage
  conversion analysis, cohort retention curves, sales
  productivity metrics, forecast accuracy discipline. This
  module has a founder who knows every deal by name; the
  peer track builds the analytics that replace that
  personal knowledge.

The rule of thumb: **anything this module treats in a
paragraph, the GTM track treats in a chapter or two.** If
you have hired an AE, if you have a marketing function, if
you are managing a quota-carrying team — the depth you need
is in `startup-product-gtm-curriculum`, not here.

## Boundary 2 — Legal counsel

Explicitly **out** of scope for this module — routed to
counsel:

- **Drafting the MSA from scratch.** The Master Service
  Agreement is a legal instrument with implications
  counsel must weigh — governing law, jurisdiction,
  liability cap language, warranty disclaimers,
  indemnification carve-outs, IP assignment. Counsel
  drafts; the founder reads, uses as the starting document
  for every deal, and negotiates redlines.
- **Drafting the DPA / BAA.** Data protection paper
  (GDPR-compliant DPA with SCCs where cross-border transfer
  is involved; HIPAA-compliant BAA where PHI is involved)
  is regulatory paper. Counsel drafts template; the
  founder maintains and customizes per deal within the
  template's rails.
  <!-- needs-research: GDPR Article 28 - Regulation (EU) 2016/679. HIPAA BAA - 45 CFR §164.502(e), §164.504(e). Standard Contractual Clauses - European Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914. -->
- **Redline decisions on non-standard buyer clauses.**
  When the buyer's redline touches a clause counsel has
  flagged as non-negotiable, the escalation is to
  counsel — for a written opinion on what the redline
  actually means and what alternatives exist. The
  founder negotiates the *commercial* trade; counsel
  advises on the *legal* implication.
- **Security and privacy compliance program design.** SOC 2
  scope and controls, ISO 27001 certification, HIPAA
  compliance program, GDPR data-mapping and DPIAs (Data
  Protection Impact Assessments), CCPA compliance. Counsel
  or a compliance specialist owns the program; the
  founder participates as data controller and executive
  sponsor.
  <!-- needs-research: SOC 2 - AICPA Trust Services Criteria. ISO 27001 - International Organization for Standardization. GDPR DPIA - Article 35, Regulation (EU) 2016/679. CCPA - California Civil Code §1798.100 et seq. -->
- **Sales-side employment paper.** Offer letters,
  commission plan documents, IP assignment clauses in
  employee agreements, non-compete/non-solicit clauses
  (where enforceable). Counsel drafts; the founder signs.
- **Regulatory disclosure and vertical-specific paper.**
  Insurance, financial services, government, healthcare
  verticals each carry their own regulatory paper
  (e.g., FINRA registration for financial services,
  FedRAMP authorization for federal government, HITRUST
  for healthcare). Counsel (often specialist counsel per
  vertical) drives the compliance path; the founder makes
  the commercial decision about entering the vertical.
- **Litigation, dispute resolution, arbitration.** Any
  contract dispute goes to counsel immediately; the
  founder does not respond to a buyer's legal complaint
  or a demand letter without counsel's opinion.

The founder-counsel split for this module mirrors the same
split from **mod-005 chapter 09** (briefing counsel on the
cap-table walk and term-sheet redline): **the founder
authors the commercial artifact and the negotiating
position; counsel authors the legal opinion.** Both are
necessary; neither substitutes for the other.

## What the founder-CEO still owes the sales function, always

The boundary transfers the *craft* to the peer tracks. It
does not transfer the *judgement* — that is permanently CEO
work. The founder-CEO's ongoing obligations, at every stage
of the company, are:

- **Own the top strategic deals.** Post-first-hire, the CEO
  personally runs the top 1–3 deals in the pipeline —
  lighthouse accounts, unusual product asks, deals that
  define the next segment. Sales leadership can run the
  playbook; only the CEO can invent the next one.
- **Own the pricing model.** Concessions above a threshold,
  custom deal structures, unusual terms all route to the
  CEO. This is the same discipline as owning the cap-table
  walk from mod-005 or the operating model from mod-003:
  the CEO does not outsource the numbers that define the
  business.
- **Own the counsel relationship for commercial paper.**
  Counsel is a service provider; the CEO decides what to
  ask counsel to do (which redlines to escalate, what
  clauses matter) and how to weigh the legal opinion
  against the business trade-off. Post-hire, a General
  Counsel or a first legal hire may partially inherit this
  in day-to-day paper flow; the CEO stays in the room for
  material terms.
- **References-sell for the sales team.** A 30-minute
  founder call with the top of the AE's pipeline is the
  fastest way to close deals for the first year after the
  first hire. The founder as *reference*, not as *closer*,
  is a permanent role.
- **Recruit sales leadership personally.** The first AE,
  first Head of Sales, first VP of Sales — all founder-
  led hires. The judgement about the profile that fits
  the specific motion does not transfer for at least a
  year and often longer.

## When to graduate from this module to the peer tracks

Three signals it's time to move the deeper sales work from
"founder does it themselves at 11pm" to the GTM track (the
same conditions as chapter 07's transition signal):

- **~$500k–$2M ARR across 5–15 named customers** —
  enough sample to see a pattern, small enough that the
  founder knows every deal.
- **A written playbook** covering the full motion — one an
  AE could execute without the founder in the room.
- **A repeatable pattern** in the last 3–5 deals — same
  segment, same buyer, same pilot, same objections, same
  cycle length.

Before those signals, this module's material is the current
work. After them, this module's material remains true — the
Mom Test discipline, the qualification rubric, the pilot
design, the paper process, the feedback loop don't stop
being right — but the operating pattern they live inside is
different, and that is what the peer track teaches.

## Cross-module boundaries in the founder-ceo track

Two adjacent boundaries within this track worth naming
explicitly:

- **Mod-002 (Lean Business Modeling)** owns the pricing
  hypothesis, the segment definition, and the unit
  economics. This module *tests* those hypotheses with
  real sales data and feeds signal back into the
  mod-002 canvas (chapter 06). The permanent home for
  pricing-as-a-strategic-artifact stays in the mod-002
  material and, at scale, the GTM track.
- **Mod-005 (Equity, SAFEs & Cap Tables) — chapter 09,
  briefing counsel** — establishes the founder/counsel
  split for cap-table paper. The same discipline governs
  sales paper: the founder authors the commercial
  artifact, counsel authors the legal opinion. The
  patterns are identical; only the paper is different.

## Summary

- This module ends at the **transition signal**: ~$500k–$2M
  ARR, 5–15 named customers, written playbook, repeatable
  pattern. After that signal, the GTM track owns the
  deeper craft.
- **`startup-product-gtm-curriculum` (level 30)** owns:
  first SDR/AE hire, quota-carrying reps, marketing
  engine, formal sales organization, channel strategy,
  customer success as a function, and pricing/packaging
  as a permanent GTM discipline.
- **Legal counsel** owns: drafting the MSA / DPA / BAA
  templates, redline decisions on non-standard clauses,
  compliance program design, sales-side employment paper,
  regulatory paper, and any dispute or litigation. The
  founder negotiates and signs; counsel drafts and reviews.
- **Craft transfers; judgement stays.** Post-hire, the CEO
  still owns the top strategic deals, the pricing model,
  the counsel relationship for material terms, references-
  sells for the AE's pipeline, and recruits sales
  leadership personally.
- The **founder/counsel split** in this module mirrors the
  split in mod-005 chapter 09: **CEO authors the
  commercial artifact and the negotiating position;
  counsel authors the legal opinion.** Same discipline,
  different paper.
