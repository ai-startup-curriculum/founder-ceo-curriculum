# Exercise 02 — Hard Qualification Drill

**Module:** 006 Founder-led Sales · **Stage:** SEED · **Time:**
~2 focused hours · **Reads with:** chapter
[03](../03-hard-qualification-and-disqualification.md).

## Problem statement

Qualification is a decision, made weekly, about every open
opportunity. The answer is "advance to pilot" or "walk away
this week." Most founder pipelines rot because that decision
is deferred into a third answer ("keep it warm") that has no
mechanism to convert.

This exercise is a **drill**: six deal scenarios that walk
you through applying MEDDPICC and BANT+C consistently, with
disqualification triggers written *before* seeing the
outcome. The goal is not to close deals in the exercise; it
is to practice the mental motion of applying the rubric hard
enough that a bad-fit deal is disqualified on evidence, not
on optimism.

## Deliverables (one filled-in drill sheet)

A single **drill sheet** (spreadsheet or Markdown table) with
your qualification decision (advance / walk away) for each
of the six scenarios, plus a one-sentence justification per
scenario naming the specific MEDDPICC or BANT+C dimension
that drove the decision. Include your pre-committed
disqualification triggers at the top of the sheet.

## Requirements

Read each scenario. For each:

1. Fill in the MEDDPICC (or BANT+C) fields with what you can
   observe from the scenario text; mark any missing
   dimensions as **NAMED-BUT-UNKNOWN** (you have to run
   more discovery) or **STRUCTURALLY-MISSING** (the buyer
   has no answer to give).
2. Check the scenario against your pre-committed
   disqualification triggers.
3. Make a decision: **advance to pilot**, **advance to
   more discovery**, or **walk away this week**.
4. Write a one-sentence justification naming the specific
   MEDDPICC/BANT+C dimension or disqualifier that drove
   the decision.

### Pre-committed disqualification triggers (fill in first)

Before reading the scenarios, write **at least 5**
disqualification triggers you commit to applying uniformly.
Examples (from chapter 03) — write your own, don't copy:

- [ ] No named economic buyer after N calls (N = ___)
- [ ] Champion cannot articulate the pain unprompted
- [ ] No budget cycle we can hit this year
- [ ] Success metric is unmeasurable or unowned
- [ ] Procurement / security review makes the pilot uneconomic
- [ ] Buyer wants a custom product build as a prerequisite
- [ ] Incumbent contract renews in 8+ months and buyer cannot
      commit to switching mid-term
- [ ] (Your own): ___
- [ ] (Your own): ___
- [ ] (Your own): ___

Write these before you read the scenarios. The point of
pre-commitment is that live-opportunity psychology is exactly
wrong for making the walk-away call cleanly (chapter 03).

## Starter guidance

**Session 1 (~45 min) — pre-commit and read the easy calls.**
Write your 5+ disqualification triggers before you read any
scenario. Then work scenarios 1, 2, and 5. These are the
scenarios where the "walk away" call should be relatively
clean; the drill is to see whether your pre-committed triggers
actually fire.

**Session 2 (~45 min) — the ambiguous ones.** Scenarios 3, 4,
and 6. These are the ones where the founder's optimism (or
the buyer's specific behaviors) muddy the call. The point of
the drill is not to be right; the point is to be *consistent*
— to make the decision on the observable evidence and the
rubric, not on the vibe.

**Session 3 (~30 min) — pattern review.** Look at your six
decisions together. Which pattern of evidence pushed you to
"walk away" fastest? Which patterns did you rationalize? If
scenario 1 and scenario 5 both look like "walk away" to you,
what do they have in common that your rubric should catch?

## Acceptance criteria

You are done when *all* of the following are true:

- [ ] At least 5 pre-committed disqualification triggers,
      written before reading any scenario.
- [ ] Every scenario has a completed MEDDPICC or BANT+C fill,
      with dimensions marked NAMED-BUT-UNKNOWN or
      STRUCTURALLY-MISSING where the scenario doesn't
      provide the answer.
- [ ] Every scenario has a decision (advance to pilot /
      advance to more discovery / walk away this week) and a
      one-sentence justification naming the specific
      dimension or trigger.
- [ ] Scenarios 1, 2, and 3 all end in "walk away" or
      "advance to more discovery, with a specific gap to
      close" — an "advance to pilot" on any of these three
      is a red flag your rubric is not being applied hard
      enough.
- [ ] Scenario 5 explicitly cites segment-fit (mod-002
      canvas) as part of the decision, not just MEDDPICC.
- [ ] Scenario 6 is the only clear "advance" — with a named
      next step, not just "we'll follow up."
- [ ] Pattern-review notes name at least one pattern of
      evidence you rationalized in one scenario and want to
      write a new disqualifier for.

## Self-assessment rubric

<!-- needs-research: exemplar for mod-006 not yet authored; rubric-vs-exemplar comparison assumes future exemplars/mod-006-founder-led-sales/. -->

| Dimension | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-commitment | Wrote triggers after reading the scenarios | Wrote 5+ triggers before opening any scenario |
| Framework consistency | Used MEDDPICC on one, BANT on another, no reason given | Named framework per deal with an explicit reason (deal size, stakeholder count) |
| Missing-dimension honesty | Filled in blanks with optimism | Marked NAMED-BUT-UNKNOWN / STRUCTURALLY-MISSING where the scenario didn't say |
| Segment-fit filter | Skipped segment-fit for enterprise scenarios | Explicitly cited mod-002 segment in scenarios 5 and 6 |
| Champion diagnostic | Treated "enthusiastic" as champion | Distinguished coach (2/5 signals) from champion (4-5/5 signals) |
| Walk-away discipline | Advanced deals that hit disqualifiers | Applied triggers uniformly, even when the logo was attractive |
| Pattern learning | Six independent decisions with no reflection | Named at least one pattern to write into next round's disqualifiers |

## Definition of done

A skeptical co-founder or an operator who has closed
enterprise deals before would read your drill sheet and
agree that (a) your disqualification triggers are specific
and observable, (b) each scenario's decision is defensible on
the evidence in the scenario (not on vibes), (c) you did not
soft-pedal the walk-away call on any big-logo or
enthusiastic-champion scenario, and (d) your pattern-review
notes identify at least one gap in your rubric that would
have caught a real deal you either wasted time on or would
have walked away from too fast.
