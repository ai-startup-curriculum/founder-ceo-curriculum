# Exercise 03 — Pilot Scope & Success Metric Authoring

**Module:** 006 Founder-led Sales · **Stage:** SEED · **Time:**
~2–3 focused hours · **Reads with:** chapter
[04](../04-pilot-scope-and-success-metric.md).

## Problem statement

A pilot without a written, pre-agreed, quantitative success
metric is a free consulting engagement the founder cannot
exit. The single most expensive pilot mistake is running a
loosely-scoped trial and hoping the buyer will convert on
their own timeline — which they never do.

This exercise is an **authoring drill**: three pilot design
scenarios where you author scope (one workflow, one team,
one metric), the success metric (pre-agreed, quantitative,
owned by their side, with baseline and target), duration
(4–8 weeks, calendar-fixed), price (paid best, free-with-
teeth second, free never), and exit criteria (success /
partial / miss). The point is to build the mental habit of
authoring these fields *before* the buyer conversation, not
during it.

## Deliverables (three completed pilot designs)

For each of the three scenarios below, a completed pilot
design document (spreadsheet, Markdown, or a lightweight
pilot-agreement template) with:

1. **Scope** — the one workflow, one team, one metric, plus
   an explicit out-of-scope list.
2. **Success metric** — pre-agreed, quantitative, owned by
   their side, with baseline, target, measurement method, and
   named reporter.
3. **Duration** — 4–8 weeks, calendar-fixed with start and
   end dates.
4. **Price** — paid, free-with-teeth, or free (with a
   justification for the choice).
5. **Exit criteria** — pre-agreed next step for success /
   partial / miss.
6. **Signers** — named signer on their side (economic buyer
   preferred, not just champion).
7. **Attribution** — whose data, whose measurement, whose
   report.

Plus one short **debrief note** (1–2 paragraphs) per pilot
answering: what is the single strongest signal a "walk away"
was warranted, and what is the single strongest signal an
"advance" was warranted?

## Requirements

Read each scenario. Author the pilot from scratch — do not
copy from the templates in chapter 04 without customizing
every field to the scenario. The mental habit is to look at
the buyer, the workflow, and the segment, and *construct* the
pilot that fits them.

### Scenario 1 — B2B ops SaaS, mid-market, quantifiable pain

*You are selling a B2B SaaS product that helps operations
teams automate manual data entry between two systems (their
CRM and their ERP). Target ACV: $80k. The prospect is a
600-person B2B services company. Champion: Director of Ops
(has done a similar procurement before). Economic buyer:
VP of Ops (confirmed budget authority, willing to sign a
pilot). Pain: an operations analyst spends ~15 hours/week
on manual data reconciliation between the two systems, and
errors from mistyped entries cost the company an estimated
2-3 hours of downstream cleanup per error, with roughly 4-5
errors per week. Their existing tooling: none dedicated;
Excel and internal scripts. Trigger event: the analyst is
leaving next quarter, and they don't want to hire a
replacement for a job that could be automated.*

Author the pilot. Success metric family: chapter 04 lists
time-saved-on-a-task, error-rate-reduction, volume-shifted,
adoption-threshold — pick the one that best fits and defend
the choice.

### Scenario 2 — Developer-tools SaaS, small team, adoption-driven

*You are selling a developer-productivity tool (per-seat SaaS,
$50/user/month, target 40-seat pilot converting to a 250-seat
contract at $12k ACV). The prospect is a 400-person software
company. Champion: Engineering Manager who advocates for
developer productivity tools. Economic buyer: VP of Engineering
(willing to sign a paid pilot up to $10k). Pain: engineers
report friction with an existing internal tool; the champion
believes your product could replace it. There is no
quantitative baseline — the "friction" is qualitative today.*

Author the pilot. The trick here is that a purely
qualitative baseline ("engineers are frustrated") is not a
metric. You must either (a) instrument a quantitative
baseline in week 1 (e.g., cycle time on a specific workflow,
error rate on a specific action) or (b) commit to a
secondary metric like adoption threshold (X% of the 40-user
cohort using the tool at least Y times/week by end of pilot)
as the primary. Defend the choice.

### Scenario 3 — Regulated vertical, HIPAA, long paper process

*You are selling a healthcare-adjacent workflow tool to a
mid-size specialty healthcare provider (2,500 employees,
covered entity under HIPAA). Champion: Chief of Staff to the
COO. Economic buyer: COO (confirmed budget authority up to
$200k ACV target). Pain: staff spend significant time on a
manual workflow that touches PHI. The pilot cannot proceed
without a signed BAA; their security review is expected to
take 60-90 days; their procurement portal onboarding is
another 30 days. You have SOC 2 Type II and a template BAA
drafted by counsel; you do not yet have HITRUST. The buyer
is willing to run a technical pilot in parallel with the
security review, on synthetic (non-PHI) data.*

Author the pilot. The challenge: you must design a pilot
whose calendar and scope work around a 60-90 day security
review, without either sitting idle for 90 days or
compromising the HIPAA obligations. Use the "synthetic-data
technical pilot in parallel with security review" structure
and define what would trigger promotion to a PHI-touching
pilot afterward.

## Starter guidance

**Session 1 (~45 min) — Scenario 1.** The straightforward
one. Success metric type: time-saved-on-a-task (~15 hours
per week baseline) or error-rate reduction (~4-5 errors per
week baseline). Baseline is measurable pre-pilot; target
should be a specific number (e.g., "reduce to <2 hours/week
of manual reconciliation," or "reduce errors to <1/week").
Duration: 4-6 weeks. Price: paid ($5-15k) because the buyer
has confirmed budget and this filters seriousness. Exit
criteria: success → order form within 10 days; partial →
2-week extension with amendment; miss → clean debrief.

**Session 2 (~45 min) — Scenario 2.** The tricky one. The
first week of the pilot is instrumenting the baseline — get
their engineering champion to name a specific quantitative
proxy for "friction" (typical: cycle time on a common
workflow, or error rate on a specific action). If they will
not instrument the baseline, that is a qualification signal
— the metric is not actually a shared priority. Fall back
to an adoption-threshold primary only if instrumentation is
truly not feasible in the pilot window; note that
adoption-threshold pilots are weaker signal because they
measure usage, not value.

**Session 3 (~60 min) — Scenario 3.** The scoping puzzle.
The pilot must have two phases: (a) a 4-6 week
synthetic-data technical validation (proves the workflow
works, no PHI touched, security review runs in parallel),
and (b) a promotion criterion — "on completion of security
review + BAA signature + [technical validation success], we
begin a 4-week PHI pilot with the [named 10-person] team."
Price: paid pilot at $10-25k for the synthetic-data phase;
the second phase is bundled into the eventual contract.
Signers: COO signs the pilot agreement, InfoSec signs the
security-review closeout, both required before phase 2.

## Acceptance criteria

You are done when *all* of the following are true, for each
of the three scenarios:

- [ ] Scope names exactly one workflow, one team, and one
      primary metric — not "the platform" or "several
      workflows."
- [ ] Out-of-scope list has at least 3 items explicitly
      listed (adjacent workflow, additional teams beyond
      one, enterprise features until contract, etc.).
- [ ] Success metric is quantitative with a specific number,
      not an adjective; has a baseline that is measurable
      pre-pilot or in the first week; has a target that
      would clearly count as "pass"; is owned by a named
      reporter on their side, not by you.
- [ ] Duration is 4–8 weeks with specific start and end
      dates. Extensions require a written amendment, not a
      Slack message.
- [ ] Price is either paid or free-with-teeth. If free-with-
      teeth, the signed pilot agreement and named
      conversion criteria are explicit. No "free" pilots.
- [ ] Signer on their side is named. If it is the champion
      rather than the economic buyer, you have a specific
      reason.
- [ ] Attribution is unambiguous — their data, their
      measurement, their reporter.
- [ ] Exit criteria name all three outcomes (success,
      partial, miss) with pre-agreed next steps for each.
- [ ] The debrief note names the strongest walk-away and
      advance signals in one sentence each.

Scenario-specific:

- [ ] **Scenario 1**: success metric is a specific hours-per-
      week or errors-per-week number, with a specific target,
      not "meaningful reduction."
- [ ] **Scenario 2**: either (a) week-1 instrumentation of a
      quantitative baseline is written into the scope, or
      (b) adoption-threshold is chosen as primary with a
      written justification.
- [ ] **Scenario 3**: the pilot has two phases (synthetic-
      data technical validation + PHI pilot post-BAA), with
      a specific promotion criterion between them.

## Self-assessment rubric

| Dimension | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Scope discipline | "Pilot the product" | One workflow, one team, one metric; out-of-scope list has 3+ specific items |
| Metric quality | Qualitative or vague ("improve efficiency") | Quantitative with specific number, baseline measurable, target unambiguous |
| Metric attribution | Founder-owned measurement | Their data, their tools, their named reporter |
| Duration discipline | Open-ended or "until it feels done" | 4-8 weeks, calendar-fixed, extension requires amendment |
| Pricing choice | Free with no agreement | Paid or free-with-teeth with signed agreement and named conversion criteria |
| Signer choice | Champion signs alone | Economic buyer signs (or champion's manager, with justification) |
| Exit criteria | Success only, or "we'll figure it out" | All three outcomes named with pre-agreed next steps |
| Scenario 1 metric | "Reduce manual work" | Specific hours-per-week or error-rate number, baseline + target |
| Scenario 2 baseline | "We'll get a baseline eventually" | Week-1 instrumentation explicit, or adoption-threshold justified as primary |
| Scenario 3 structure | Single-phase pilot ignoring HIPAA constraints | Two-phase pilot with promotion criterion, HIPAA-safe synthetic phase first |

## Definition of done

A skeptical co-founder or an operator who has closed
enterprise pilots before would read your three pilot designs
and agree that (a) each pilot answers one clear question with
a pre-agreed quantitative metric owned by the buyer, (b) each
has a calendar-fixed end date that forces a commercial
conversation, (c) none are structured as free-with-no-teeth
consulting engagements, and (d) scenario 3's two-phase
structure demonstrates you can design a pilot that works
around a real regulatory constraint without either giving up
or breaking the rules.
