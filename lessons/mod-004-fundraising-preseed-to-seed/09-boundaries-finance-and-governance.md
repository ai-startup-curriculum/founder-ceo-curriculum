# Chapter 09 — Boundaries: This Module Owns the Pre-Seed → Seed Raise; Peer Tracks Own Post-Series-A Depth and Board Governance

> **Reads with:** module objectives 9 and 10 — *cite the boundary to
> `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` (level 30) for
> post-Series-A fundraising depth, secondary transactions, and
> CFO-scale fundraising process craft; cite the boundary to
> `startup-operations-governance-curriculum` (level 50) for board
> management / composition / fiduciary duty.*

## Why the boundaries exist

Founder/CEO curriculum modules are role pathways over shared
functional curricula, not a separate body of knowledge (see the
top-level `README.md` and `PREREQUISITES.md`). The founder learns
*enough of every function to run the company through the seed
round*; the deeper craft of each function is owned by the pathway
for the role that will eventually do it full-time. Fundraising and
governance follow the same rule.

- **This module (`founder-ceo/mod-004-fundraising-preseed-to-seed`)**
  teaches you to design and run **the pre-seed → seed raise**: the
  ladder, the amount tied to a milestone, the investor funnel, the
  parallel time-boxed process, the lead-follow structure, the
  stage-matched pitch, the signal/theater discipline, and the
  close.
- **The startup-finance-fundraising track
  (`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`, level 30)** teaches
  the deeper craft of **post-Series-A fundraising, secondary
  transactions, and CFO-scale fundraising process craft** — the
  work a CFO and a Head of Corporate Development do once the
  company is large enough that a round is a multi-quarter,
  multi-workstream project with a dedicated finance function
  running it alongside the CEO.
- **The startup-operations-governance track
  (`startup-operations-governance-curriculum`, level 50)** teaches
  **board management, composition, and fiduciary duty** — the work
  the founder-CEO does *with* the board after a lead's board seat
  is instantiated: running effective board meetings, composing an
  independent director search, discharging fiduciary duty as a
  director, and running the CEO / board-lead relationship over
  years.

If you are the founder-CEO of a pre-Series-A company, most of
what this module covers is your job today (with a lawyer, a
fractional CFO, or an operator-mentor filling whatever slice you
can't reach yourself). Once you land the seed and hire a first
finance leader, and once you have a real board, the deeper craft
transfers — but the material in this module remains the founder's
baseline understanding of *how the raise happens* and *what a
board seat is*.

## What "the pre-seed → seed raise" covers, and what it deliberately doesn't

This module is scoped to the founder artifact that carries every
seed-stage financing decision: **the operating plan that fell out

closes**.

- The **investor funnel** — segmented targets, stage-by-stage

## The boundary to `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` (level 30)

Explicitly **out** of scope for this module — deferred to the
startup-finance-fundraising track:

- **Post-Series-A fundraising process craft.** Series B, C, and
  later; growth rounds; crossover and pre-IPO rounds; the
  multi-workstream project management of a large round; the
  CFO-and-CEO division of labour that runs a modern venture round.
  The parallel, time-boxed discipline from
  [chapter 04](./04-parallel-time-boxed-process.md) still applies
  in shape; the scale, the paperwork, and the internal team
  supporting the round are all different.
- **Secondary transactions.** Structured secondary sales, tender
  offers, employee liquidity events, and the tax, accounting, and
  cap-table mechanics of each. Founder-scale material in this
  module doesn't touch secondaries; they belong to the finance
  track.

- **Venture debt, revenue-based financing, and non-dilutive
  capital.** SVB / venture-lender terms, covenants, warrant
  coverage; RBF as an alternative to venture equity; grant
  capital, SBIR, and adjacent non-dilutive tools. Referenced from
  this module only where a founder needs to know they exist as
  alternatives.
  <!-- needs-research: primary-source references for standard venture debt structures; SVB's public writing and the National Venture Capital Association (NVCA) publications are common anchors. -->
- **Formal financial-diligence workstreams.** Quality of Earnings
  reports, ASC 606 revenue-recognition diligence, working-capital
  bridges, purchase-price adjustments — the finance-side
  diligence work that supports a Series B+ round or an M&A
  transaction.
  <!-- needs-research: primary-source references for ASC 606 (FASB) — the current authoritative citation for revenue-recognition standards in the US. -->
- **M&A process craft.** Sell-side and buy-side transaction
  processes; strategic-acquirer negotiations; earn-outs; escrows;
  reps and warranties insurance. Adjacent to fundraising but its
  own body of craft.
- **Detailed term-sheet negotiation at Series B+.** Full
  liquidation preference stacks, participating vs. non-participating
  preferred, protective provisions across multiple series, drag-along
  and tag-along interactions at scale, redemption rights,
  anti-dilution mechanics beyond broad-based weighted-average.
  Mod-005 and this module cover the seed-level version; the
  finance track owns the depth.
- **Investor relations as a permanent discipline.** Quarterly
  investor letters, annual meetings, LP-facing reporting,
  investor-database management, PR strategy for financings. Once
  the cap table is larger and the reporting cadence is real, IR
  becomes its own function.

The rule of thumb: **anything this module treats in a paragraph,
the finance track treats in a chapter or two.** If you are hiring
the CFO who will run the Series B, or if you are the CFO reading
this module to align with the founder, the depth you need is in
`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`, not here.

## The boundary to `startup-operations-governance-curriculum` (level 50)

Explicitly **out** of scope for this module — deferred to the
operations-and-governance track:

- **Running the board as an operating instrument.** Board-meeting
  cadence, agenda design, the standard board deck (metrics
  section, strategic section, ask section), pre-reads, executive
  sessions, and the "board work happens between meetings" reality
  that makes any given meeting valuable.
  <!-- needs-research: primary-source references for standard board-meeting mechanics; Fred Wilson's *AVC* and Brad Feld's *Startup Boards: Getting the Most Out of Your Board of Directors* (Wiley, 2013; 2nd ed. planned) are canonical, but current stable URLs vary. -->
- **Board composition over time.** When to add an independent
  director; how to source and vet independents; the mechanics of
  a board-observer role; the seat-count math as new investors
  join across rounds; the founder / lead / independent / employee
  balance and how it evolves.
- **Fiduciary duty as a director.** The duty of care and duty of
  loyalty owed by directors under Delaware corporate law; the
  business-judgment rule; conflict-of-interest management; the
  specific fiduciary shifts that occur in zone-of-insolvency
  situations. This is the material a founder-director must know
  by the time they sit on their own company's board.
  <!-- needs-research: primary-source references for Delaware corporate-law fiduciary duty — the Delaware General Corporation Law (Title 8, Delaware Code) is the statutory anchor; leading Delaware Chancery cases (e.g., *Trados*, *Revlon*, *In re Caremark*) shape the case law. -->
- **CEO / board-lead relationship management.** The 1:1s outside
  board meetings; the "how to disagree with your lead partner
  without breaking the relationship" work; the founder-to-CEO
  transition (or founder-departure) conversation; the
  chair-of-the-board role once it exists.
- **Executive compensation as a board-approved artefact.**
  Compensation committees, 409A valuations at board scale, option
  refresh plans, hiring executive compensation with board
  approval, the CEO's own compensation package as a
  board-approved item.
  <!-- needs-research: primary-source references for IRC §409A and the mechanics of 409A valuations; IRS regulations under §409A and standard 409A-valuation methodology (income / market / asset approaches) are the anchors. -->
- **Committee structure.** Audit committee, compensation
  committee, nominating/governance committee — when each becomes
  necessary and what each formally owes to the board.
- **Board-adjacent legal artefacts.** D&O insurance policy design;
  written consent procedures; unanimous-written-consent norms;
  the corporate secretary function.
- **Investor updates as a governance instrument** (as distinct
  from investor updates as a fundraising asset covered in
  [chapter 08](./08-running-the-close.md)). The monthly-or-quarterly
  update as a signaling and accountability tool that a good board
  reads carefully.

The rule of thumb: **this module owns the raise; the operations-
and-governance track owns what comes after the money lands.** The
lead's board seat is *instantiated* by the close covered in
[chapter 08](./08-running-the-close.md); how it is *used* — by the
founder and by the director — is the operations-and-governance
track's craft.

## When to graduate from this module to the peer tracks

Three signals it's time to move the deeper work from "founder
does it themselves at 11pm" to the appropriate peer track:

- **First real finance hire in seat.** A Head of Finance / CFO
  changes what the founder should own personally. Hand off the
  round mechanics, the investor-relations cadence, and the model
  work as the starting corpus; the CFO will rebuild in their own
  tooling. From that point, the finance track's material is the
  standard the finance function is being held to.
- **Round-size crossing roughly $10M–$15M or Series A+.** The
  paperwork, the diligence workstream, and the CFO involvement
  scale up meaningfully. A Series A run out of this module alone
  is legal; a Series B run out of this module alone is
  under-prepared.
- **Instantiation of a real board with independent directors.**
  Once your board is more than the founders and the lead
  investor, the governance track's material becomes
  load-bearing. Board mechanics that a friendly seed lead
  tolerated turn into problems with an independent director in
  the room.

Before those signals, this module's material is sufficient. After
them, this module's material remains true — the ladder, the
funnel arithmetic, the pitch/stage match, the signal/theater
discipline, the close discipline don't stop being right — but
the operating pattern they live inside is different, and that is
what the peer tracks teach.

## What the founder-CEO still owes the fundraising and governance functions, always

The boundary transfers the *craft* to the peer tracks. It does
not transfer the *judgment* — that is permanently CEO work. The
founder-CEO's ongoing obligations, at every stage of the company,
are:

- **Own the raise decision.** No matter how large the finance
  function grows, the CEO who cannot state "we are raising $X by
  \[date\] to buy \[milestone\]" from memory has outsourced
  something no CFO should own alone.
- **Own the lead-partner relationship.** The CFO can run process;
  the CEO owns the trust with the lead partner. That does not
  transfer.
- **Own the pitch.** The narrative can be co-authored; the
  delivery cannot be delegated. Series A partners still expect the
  CEO in the room.
- **Own the board.** The operations-and-governance track will
  teach board mechanics in depth; the CEO's ownership of the
  board relationship — agenda, cadence, information flow — is
  permanent.
- **Refuse to let the process become opaque.** A finance function
  producing beautiful investor materials that the CEO doesn't
  personally understand is a dangerous condition. The CEO
  periodically re-deriving the raise math and the funnel math
  from first principles is a permanent responsibility.

## Summary

- This module ends at the **pre-seed → seed raise** — the ladder,
  the raise sized off the mod-003 model against a milestone, the
  segmented funnel, the parallel time-boxed process, the
  lead-follow structure, the stage-matched pitch, the
  signal/theater discipline, and the close.
- **Post-Series-A fundraising depth, secondary transactions, and
  CFO-scale fundraising process craft** belong to the
  **`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` (level 30)** peer
  track. Defer to it once a finance function exists or the round
  size crosses roughly $10M–$15M.
- **Board management, composition, and fiduciary duty** belong to
  the **`startup-operations-governance-curriculum` (level 50)**
  peer track. Defer to it once a real board with independent
  directors is instantiated.
- Transfer *craft* to the peer tracks; keep *judgment* (the raise
  decision, the lead-partner relationship, the pitch, the board
  relationship) permanently with the CEO.
