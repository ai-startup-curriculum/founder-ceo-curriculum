# Module 004 — Fundraising: Pre-seed → Seed

> **Stage:** PRE-SEED→SEED · **Pillar:** Fundraising · **Prereq:**
> mod-003

Fundraising feels like a story problem, and founders who treat it
that way end up depending on luck. The founders who close reliably
treat it as a **pipeline problem** — one where the story matters,
but where the number of term sheets that fall out the bottom is a
mechanical function of how many targets went in the top and how
well each stage converted.

The math is unforgiving. If your funnel converts 20% target → first
meeting, 25% first meeting → partner meeting, and 20% partner
meeting → term sheet, a list of 50 investors returns, on average,
**half** a term sheet. That is not a round; that is a coin flip. A
list of 200, run in parallel over 6–8 weeks with warm intros and
momentum, returns two or three term sheets — enough to pick a
partner, not just take the only offer you got.

The other failure mode is raising against a vibe instead of a
model. Mod-003 built the operating plan; this module raises against
it. The right size of the round, the right stage to raise at, and
the right thing to promise investors all fall out of the zero-cash
date and the milestone the round is supposed to buy. A raise that
isn't tied to a milestone is a raise that never ends.

Deeper post-Series-A fundraising process craft, secondary
transactions, and CFO-scale round management is deferred to the
`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` peer track; board
management, composition, and fiduciary duty are deferred to
`startup-operations-governance-curriculum`. Both handoffs are made
explicit in
[chapter 09](./09-boundaries-finance-and-governance.md).

## Learning objectives

After this module you can:

1. Describe the **fundraising ladder** — angels ($10k–$100k
   individual checks) → pre-seed ($500k–$3M from pre-seed funds
   like Hustle Fund / Precursor / K9) → seed ($2M–$5M traditional,
   $5M–$15M "big seed") → Series A ($8M–$25M) — including what
   each stage expects from the founder in exchange.
2. **Compute how much to raise** from the mod-003 operating model,
   tied to a specific 12–24 month milestone, with the resulting
   seed dilution range (~15–25%) stated.
3. Design an **investor funnel** with segmented targets (stage
   focus, sector fit, check-size / lead behaviour, warm-path
   score) and named conversion assumptions at each stage
   (target → first meeting ~15–25%, first → partner meeting
   ~20–35%, partner → term sheet ~15–25%).
4. Run a round as a **parallel, time-boxed process** — practice
   batch first, A-tier in parallel with a hard close date —
   rather than sequentially, and understand why sequential leaks
   the round.
5. Distinguish the **lead-follow structure** of a round — the lead
   is the single hardest yes; followers stack allocation on the
   same terms — from a **party round** of stacked SAFEs with no
   formal lead.
6. Match the **pitch to the stage** — pre-seed sells the
   founder-and-wedge; seed sells the story plus early evidence;
   Series A sells repeatability (retention curves, cohort
   economics, working acquisition channel).
7. Distinguish **signal from theater** in investor meetings — a
   concrete second-meeting invite / reference-call schedule /
   data-room request is signal; "keep us posted" and week-plus
   radio silence are soft no's dressed as compliments.
8. Run the **close** — hold the pipeline warm until the wire
   clears, bank the round in a scheduled window with a hard close
   date, and email the passers when the round closes.
9. Locate the **boundary to `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`**
   (level 30) — this module owns the pre-seed → seed raise; the
   peer track owns post-Series-A fundraising depth, secondary
   transactions, and CFO-scale fundraising process craft.
10. Locate the **boundary to `startup-operations-governance-curriculum`**
    (level 50) — this module owns the raise; the peer track owns
    board management, composition, and fiduciary duty (what
    happens after the money lands).

## Exercises

The exercises are designed as a progression: first size the raise
against your mod-003 model, then build and segment the funnel,
then run the meeting-triage discipline that keeps the funnel
honest.

## Live-lab note

If you're an operating founder using this as a live lab: build the
funnel against your **actual** target list this quarter — the fund
names, the specific partners, the specific warm intros you can
plausibly get. The scenario numbers in the exercises are a
scaffold, but the point of the work is a pipeline for a real
raise. Depend on mod-003: you raise against a model, not a vibe.
