# Exercise 01 — Investor Funnel from 200 Targets

**Module:** 004 Fundraising: Pre-seed → Seed · **Stage:** PRE-SEED→SEED ·
**Time:** ~1 week (≈6–8 focused hours)

## Deliverables (three founder artifacts)

1. **A segmented target list of ~200 investors** — organized into tiers by
   stage fit, sector fit, check-size behavior, and warm-path strength. Each
   row identifies the fund, the specific partner, and how you'd reach them.
2. **A funnel model** — named conversion assumptions at each stage
   (target → first meeting → partner meeting → term sheet), the arithmetic
   applied to the segmented list, and the implied number of term sheets.
3. **A one-paragraph "riskiest assumption" call** — the single assumption in
   the funnel (conversion rate, warm-path coverage, round-size feasibility)
   that you'd most want to be wrong about, plus the cheapest experiment that
   would tell you whether it holds this quarter.

## Scenario

Pick one:

- **Your real startup** (recommended if you're an operating founder): use
  your **actual** round size (from your mod-003 model), your real target
  list, and the specific warm intros you can plausibly get this quarter.
- **Simulated:** You've built the mod-003 operating plan and it says you
  need to raise a **$3M seed** to buy 18 months of runway and reach the
  milestone of *"10 paying design partners at ≥ $3k ARPA."* Your current
  network gets you strong warm intros to roughly **15 seed funds** and
  weaker paths to roughly **60 more**. The remaining ~125 targets on your
  list will need to be reached via colder channels (portfolio-founder cold
  intros, LinkedIn, conference intros, or investor-scout syndicates).

## Steps

1. **Restate the raise, from mod-003.** Round size, milestone the round
   buys, target close date, months of runway post-close. Write this as one
   line at the top of the sheet. Every downstream number should be
   consistent with it.
2. **Draft the 200-target list.** Build a table with columns: fund, partner,
   stage focus (pre-seed / seed / A+), sector fit (recent adjacent
   investment: yes/no + example), typical check as lead ($), typical check
   as follow ($), warm-path source (name), and a warm-path score (1–9;
   see the module README for anchors). If you can't reach 200 with a
   credible partner name and a warm-path guess, that itself is a finding —
   you may need to invest a week in network-building before the raise
   starts.
3. **Segment into tiers.** A-tier: strong fit + warm path ≥ 6. B-tier: good
   fit, warm path 3–5. C-tier: long-shot fit or cold path. Count each
   tier. A common healthy distribution is ~15–25 A-tier, ~50–80 B-tier,
   and the rest C-tier. If your A-tier is under 10, your warm-network gap
   is the first problem to solve, not the funnel.
4. **Set conversion assumptions per tier.** Don't apply one flat conversion
   to all 200. A-tier converts target → first meeting at **50–70%**
   (warm intros land); B-tier at **15–25%**; C-tier at **1–5%**. The
   downstream stages (first meeting → partner meeting → term sheet) are
   less tier-dependent — apply the module heuristics (20–35% and 15–25%)
   or your own defensible numbers. Cite the source or mark **ASSUMPTION**
   for every rate.
5. **Compute the implied term sheets.** For each tier, apply the four-stage
   conversion to the tier's count. Sum across tiers. If the number is less
   than **~2**, the funnel is too small to produce a real choice; either
   the list needs to grow, the warm-path work needs to happen before the
   round starts, or the target round size needs to come down.
6. **Sequence the batches.** Which 10–20 investors do you pitch in the
   practice batch (weak-fit, low-cost "no"s)? Which A-tier investors do
   you open in parallel in week 2? What is your target date for having
   a soft-circled lead? Put this on the calendar, not the wish list.
7. **Name the riskiest assumption.** Is it your A-tier conversion (warm
   intros might not be as warm as you're rating them)? Is it your round
   size (a $3M seed might be a stretch for the traction you have)? Is it
   partner-meeting conversion (the deck might not survive a partnership
   review)? Which one, if it comes in at half your assumption, breaks the
   round?
8. **Design the cheapest test.** A pre-round *test-the-pitch* batch of 5
   B-tier investors validates the deck without burning A-tier intros. A
   sprint of warm-intro requests to your top 15 tier-A funds this week
   validates whether the warm-path count is real. A conversation with 2–3
   founders who recently raised the same size validates whether the round
   size is plausible for the evidence you have. Pick one, run it before
   the round officially opens.

## Rubric (self-assess, then compare to the exemplar)

| Dimension | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Round sizing | Amount picked from vibes | Round size falls out of the mod-003 model and buys a specific milestone |
| Target list | "VCs" as a category | ~200 named funds + named partners, sourced from portfolio research |
| Segmentation | One flat list | Tiered by stage fit, sector fit, check behavior, warm-path score |
| Warm-path realism | Every intro scored an 8 | Honest scoring with named intro sources; A-tier count is defensible |
| Conversion assumptions | One rate for the whole funnel | Tier-specific rates, each cited or marked ASSUMPTION |
| Implied outcomes | "We'll get some meetings" | Term-sheet count computed; ≥ 2 sheets and ≥ 1 lead required to proceed |
| Batching / timing | "We'll pitch everyone" | Practice batch, parallel A-tier open, dated close, soft-lead target |
| Riskiest assumption | Not identified | One named input + a cheap experiment that would change the call |

## Definition of done

A skeptical seed-stage investor or a founder who has run a raise recently
would read your funnel and agree that (a) the target list is real, not
aspirational, (b) the conversion assumptions are defensible for your
warm-path strength and sector, and (c) the funnel implies enough term
sheets — including at least one credible lead — to justify opening the
round.
