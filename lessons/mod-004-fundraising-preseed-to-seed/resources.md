## Books (primary references)

- **Brad Feld & Jason Mendelson, *Venture Deals: Be Smarter Than
  Your Lawyer and Venture Capitalist* (Wiley; 4th ed. 2019, 5th
  ed. 2023).** The reference on the mechanics of a round —
  term-sheet economics, option-pool math, control provisions, lead
  behaviour, and the trades hidden inside standard preferred-stock
  documents. Directly informs the ladder-and-trade framing in
  [chapter 01](./01-fundraising-ladder.md), the lead-follow
  structure in [chapter 05](./05-lead-follow-vs-party-round.md),
  and the close discipline in
  [chapter 08](./08-running-the-close.md).
  <!-- needs-research: ISBN and stable URL for Venture Deals, 5th edition. -->
- **Brad Feld & Mahendra Ramsinghani, *Startup Boards: Getting the
  Most Out of Your Board of Directors* (Wiley, 2013; 2nd ed.
  announced).** The reference on what a board seat *is* — relevant
  background for understanding what founders trade at the seed and
  Series A rungs ([chapter 01](./01-fundraising-ladder.md)).
  Governance craft itself is deferred to the operations-and-
  governance peer track (see
  [chapter 09](./09-boundaries-finance-and-governance.md)).
  <!-- needs-research: ISBN and stable URL for Startup Boards, current edition. -->
- **Scott Kupor, *Secrets of Sand Hill Road: Venture Capital and
  How to Get It* (Portfolio, 2019).** The partner-side view of how
  a VC fund actually operates — the fund's math, the partner-
  meeting dynamic, the term-sheet negotiation, the LP relationship.
  Useful for the signal-vs-theater reading in
  [chapter 07](./07-signal-vs-theater.md) because it names what the
  partner is actually trying to decide in the meeting.

- **Ben Horowitz, *The Hard Thing About Hard Things* (Harper
  Business, 2014).** Operator perspective on the raise and the

- **Chris Dixon, Peter Thiel, Marc Andreessen, and other
  practitioner anthologies.** Published collections of VC-side
  writing on round structure, pitch discipline, and term-sheet
  mechanics. Treat as context, not canon.

## Notes on citation hygiene

Where a specific claim in this module cites a fact that would
benefit from a primary source and I could not verify one at
authoring time, the chapter has an inline `<!-- needs-research: ... -->`
comment marking the gap. These do not block reading; they mark
items for later verification and block the automated merge
pipeline until resolved. If you spot a citation that should be
present or a source that has been superseded, opening an issue
against this repository is the fastest way to fix it.

Round sizes, valuations, dilution bands, and process-timing
norms in this module reflect roughly the last several US-market
cycles. They move with the venture cycle and vary by geography
and sector; treat the ranges as the *shape* of the ladder, not
as lookup values. The Carta and PitchBook-NVCA data references
above are the empirical anchors to re-check against the current
cycle.
