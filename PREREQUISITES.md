# Prerequisites for the Founder / CEO track

This curriculum is a **role pathway** at level 20 in the Startup Leadership
family. It does not re-teach the material that lower-level tracks already own.
Come in with the assumed depth below, or work through the linked prerequisite
track first.

## Assumed lower-level tracks

The pathway assumes you are fluent with, or actively working through:

- **[`startup-foundations`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-foundations)**
  — shared prerequisite for the entire startup-track ecosystem. Company
  formation, US Delaware C-corp basics, stage vocabulary (`IDEA → PRE-SEED →
  SEED → SERIES-A → GROWTH → MATURE`), the FUNCTIONAL_CURRICULA pillar map,
  and unit-economics fundamentals.

## Working knowledge you should bring

Independent of the track above, the reader should arrive with:

- A concrete idea or an operating startup to run the material against. Every
  module ends in a **founder artifact** produced for a real (or realistic
  simulated) company; the material is unusable in the abstract.
- Basic numeracy — comfort with a spreadsheet, percentage arithmetic, and the
  vocabulary of a P&L. The modules do not assume prior finance or accounting
  training but do assume you can build a month-by-month cash model when walked
  through it.
- Willingness to be honest about which stage your startup is actually at. The
  material is stage-tagged (`IDEA → PRE-SEED → SEED`); running the mod-004
  fundraising material at the pre-discovery phase will feel unmoored.
- Comfort reading foundational operator texts before or during the modules —
  Rob Fitzpatrick's *The Mom Test*, Ash Maurya's *Running Lean*, and Brad
  Feld & Jason Mendelson's *Venture Deals* are referenced in the module
  material and quoted at points.

## Not taught here

- **The CTO's core job** — technical strategy, architecture under uncertainty,
  build-vs-buy, first engineering hires, technical debt as portfolio, scaling
  the org 0→5→50 engineers, technical due diligence, founder-scope security
  and compliance. Defers to
  [`cto-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/cto-curriculum)
  (level 25). The CEO learns *how to work with a CTO* here, not how to be one.
- **Deep product discovery, roadmap construction, and experimentation craft.**
  Defers to
  [`cpo-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/cpo-curriculum).
  The CEO consumes enough discovery (mod-001) to test a first hypothesis; the
  CPO track owns discovery as a permanent function.
- **Founding-team formation, first hires, comp bands, employment paperwork,
  and deep HR / people-ops.** Defers to
  [`startup-operations-governance-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-operations-governance-curriculum)
  (level 50, People pillar). Technical hiring (founding engineer, engineer
  comp bands) is owned at level 25 by `cto-curriculum`.
- **Board composition, fiduciary duty, board management, board packets,
  cofounder-dispute mechanics, and governance.** Defers to
  [`startup-operations-governance-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-operations-governance-curriculum)
  (level 50, Governance pillar). The CEO closes the round here (mod-004);
  what to do with the board that arrives with the money lives at L50.
- **Post-Series-A fundraising depth, secondary transactions, deep FP&A /
  three-statement modeling / financial controls.** Defers to
  [`startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum)
  (level 30). The CEO owns the pre-seed → seed raise here (mod-004) and the
  founder-scale cap table (mod-005); the peer track owns the CFO-scale
  discipline.
- **Post-founder-led GTM — first SDR/AE hire, quota-carrying reps, marketing
  engine, formal sales org, channel strategy.** Defers to
  [`startup-product-gtm-curriculum`](https://github.com/ai-startup-curriculum/startup-product-gtm-curriculum)
  (level 30). The CEO sells personally through the first ~$500k–$2M ARR here
  (mod-006); the peer track owns what happens after the first sales hire.
- **M&A, acquisition, secondary transactions, and exit-time cap-table
  mechanics.** Defers to `startup-exit-curriculum` (level 40).
- **Enterprise-scale AI executive leadership** (regulatory strategy across
  frontier-lab commitments, EU AI Act enterprise programs, cross-functional AI
  strategy at 500+ employees). Defers to `chief-ai-officer` (level 70).
- **Legal opinion.** The CEO briefs counsel with a defensible package and
  signs the paper; counsel delivers opinion. The modules point out which
  clauses (liquidation preference, anti-dilution, pool refresh, IP
  assignment) are load-bearing enough that the founder should read them
  before counsel calls with a redline.
- **External audit opinion.** The CEO owns SOC 2 / ISO 27001 readiness
  posture (routed to the CTO in practice at pre-seed → seed); the CPA firm
  delivers the attest opinion.
- **Specialist advisory opinion** — patent counsel, immigration counsel, tax
  counsel. The CEO retains and briefs; the specialist delivers opinion.

## Recommended reading before you start

The modules cite these throughout; a first pass beforehand pays back
compounding leverage:

- Rob Fitzpatrick — *The Mom Test* (canonical short book on discovery
  interviews; referenced in mod-001)
- Steve Blank — *The Four Steps to the Epiphany* (customer development
  origins; referenced in mod-001)
- Teresa Torres — *Continuous Discovery Habits* (referenced in mod-001)
- Ash Maurya — *Running Lean* (3rd ed., O'Reilly; canonical lean-canvas
  book; referenced in mod-002)
- Alexander Osterwalder & Yves Pigneur — *Business Model Generation*
  (the original Business Model Canvas; referenced in mod-002)
- David Skok — *For Entrepreneurs* blog (canonical SaaS metrics posts on
  CAC, LTV, and the LTV:CAC ratio; referenced in mod-002)
- Paul Graham — *Default Alive or Default Dead?* (October 2015 essay;
  referenced in mod-003) and *How to Raise Money* / *How to Convince
  Investors* (September 2013 essays; referenced in mod-004)
- Y Combinator — *A Guide to Seed Fundraising* (Geoff Ralston; referenced
  in mod-004) and *SAFE Financing Documents* (ycombinator.com/documents;
  referenced in mod-005)
- Brad Feld & Jason Mendelson — *Venture Deals* (term-sheet reference;
  referenced in mod-004 and mod-005)
- Fred Wilson — *AVC* (avc.com; twenty years of essays on lead/follow
  structure, cap tables, and dilution)
- Carta — *State of Private Markets* quarterly reports and *Cap Table 101*
  (primary data on check sizes, round sizes, valuations, and dilution by
  stage)
- Ben Horowitz — *The Hard Thing About Hard Things* (CEO-craft reference)
- Y Combinator Startup School Library — [`startupschool.org/library`](https://www.startupschool.org/library)
- National Venture Capital Association — *Model Legal Documents*
  ([nvca.org/model-legal-documents](https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents))
  — the industry-standard priced-round templates most seed and Series A
  rounds start from; referenced in mod-005
