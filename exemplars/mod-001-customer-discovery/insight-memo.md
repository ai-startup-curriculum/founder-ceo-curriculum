# Insight Memo (Artifact #2) — Worked Exemplar

**Sprint:** 16 interviews with recently-raising technical founders.
**Problem hypothesis under test:** *technical founders are overwhelmed by
simultaneous, unordered exposure to fundraising, corporate law, equity, and GTM,
and lack a map of what to learn when.*

## What the evidence said

**Pattern 1 — The firehose is real and near-universal (14/16).** Unprompted, 14 of
16 described month one as "everything at once with no order." Representative:
*"I closed the SAFE, incorporated in Delaware, and got a partnership offer in the
same week and had no idea which mattered or what I was agreeing to."*

**Pattern 2 — They already spend time and money to cope (12/16).** Prior spend is
the strongest signal a problem is real. Twelve had already paid: fractional CFOs
($2–5k/mo), YC library binges, a $1,200 legal-templates subscription, "an advisor
mostly to tell me what to ignore." They are *already buying* a map.

**Pattern 3 — The pain has a quantified cost (9/16).** Nine could name a concrete
cost: a bad SAFE cap term ("cost me ~4 points of dilution I didn't understand"),
two months lost chasing a partnership that couldn't close, an option pool sized
wrong before a priced round.

**Pattern 4 — What they wanted was ordering, not more content (11/16).** Asked for
the one thing they'd have wanted solved, 11 said some version of *"tell me what to
ignore right now."* The scarce good is **sequence and stage-appropriateness**, not
information — information is abundant and that's the problem.

## Surprises / disconfirming evidence

- **2/16 did not feel overwhelmed** — both were second-time founders. Signal: the
  segment should be *first-time* technical founders; experience is the real axis.
- **Nobody asked for a "course."** They asked for a map, a checklist, a "what
  matters at my stage." A curriculum framed as coursework may miss; framed as a
  staged, dependency-ordered map it hits. This directly shaped the three-axis model.
- One warned that **static content goes stale** (law, fundraising norms) — implies
  freshness/review matters, not one-time authoring.

## Decision: **GO (with a sharpened problem framing)**

The problem is real, urgent, and already funded by the segment. **Proceed**, with
two adjustments the evidence forces:

1. **Sharpen the segment** to *first-time* technical founders.
2. **Frame the product as a stage-ordered dependency map** ("what matters now,
   what to ignore"), not a content library — the scarce good is sequence.

### Evidence that would change this call

- If prior-spend had been rare (<¼ of interviews), this would drop to a nice-to-have → **kill/park**.
- If "ordering" hadn't emerged as the dominant ask, the three-axis (stage × pillar
  × pathway) design would be over-built → **pivot** to a simpler checklist.
- If most overwhelm came from second-time founders too, the "first-time" segment
  cut would be wrong → **re-segment**.

> Method note: the decision rests on *counted patterns and prior spend across the
> set*, not the single best quote — that's the bar for a strong synthesis.
